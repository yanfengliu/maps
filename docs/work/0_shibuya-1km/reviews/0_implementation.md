## Authored reports

### Codex network reviewer

**F0 — P1: Turn-only tags sever internal road continuation.** `tools/network/generate.ts:156` applies the way's `turn:lanes` permissions to every generated connection, including continuation into the next segment of the same source way. `segmentsFor` creates such segments at intermediate traffic-signal and shared nodes. These tags describe lane destinations at the turn, not a requirement to turn at every intermediate graph split. In the real candidate, both `lane:1071659161:0:0:ground0:f:0` and `:f:1` have no successor at the internal split, although the same way's segment 1 continues and has outgoing turns. Way `1087233628` likewise loses both right-only lanes at segment 0. Its `:f:2:section2` and `:f:3:section2` end with empty `nextIds`. Boundary entry `lane:1377702569:1:0:ground0:r:0` is also cut before its same-way continuation by `turn:lanes:backward=right|right`. Excluding that portal from demand does not fix the builder error. Preserve internal continuation while applying destination restrictions at the relevant road movement, and test intermediate signal/shared-node splits in both travel directions. Recompute the reachability diagnostics and correct any interpretation of builder-induced disconnection as a source limitation. A read-only synthetic three-node one-way road with an intermediate traffic signal and `turn:lanes=right` also reproduced an internal dead end. Existing restriction tests do not cover this class.

**F1 — P1: Conflict splitting removes useful approach lane changes and retains junction lane changes.** `tools/network/generate.ts:431` keeps lateral IDs only when an entire original edge remains one piece. That is unrelated to whether the resulting piece is a legal lane-change section. The generated graph has 208 edges with lateral links; 114 of them have a non-null `junctionId`. For example, the roughly 6.05 m sections `lane:1298036687:0:0:ground0:r:0` and `:r:1` are inside `junction:walk:215956002:0:0:ground0` yet permit switching between each other. Conversely, the four parallel approach sections `lane:1087233628:0:0:ground0:f:{0,1,2,3}:section1` are 92.87–97.21 m long, outside every junction, and all have null neighbours. The schema promises neighbours are null across junctions, and the contract permits lane changes only through those IDs. As frozen, a faithful MOBIL consumer loses useful approach changes and gains forbidden junction changes. Rebuild lateral links for corresponding overlapping outside-conflict sections and remove them from every conflict section. Gate direction, overlap and reciprocity independently; the current validator checks only neighbour existence, kind and source way.

**F2 — P1: The rendering contract loses physical markings and signal provenance.** `src/world/network-data.ts:40` and `:44` retain only the broad walking kind and simulation controller. `tools/network/generate.ts:180` and `:209` collapse source crossing detail into those fields. Of the 208 forward crossing segments in this candidate, 21 explicitly have OSM `crossing=unmarked` or `crossing:markings=no`; only 67 carry `crossing=traffic_signals` on the source way. The remainder must not be assumed to have or lack physical signals. Near the scramble, `walk:1335178883:{0,1}:0:ground0:f` is explicitly unmarked in OSM but becomes a 5 m crossing controlled by the scramble signal. Ways `977916824` and `1335178868` provide more nearby unmarked examples. None of the 100 junction records distinguishes a real signal location from inferred simulation authority. Therefore neither a controller nor `kind=crossing` is sufficient authority for poles or zebra paint. Preserve the required physical source facts, or provide a separate attributed decoration input, and make inferred simulation control distinct from physical placement. The contract already states that timings and envelopes are authored; the missing distinction is in the consumer data.

The supplied hero frame also shows pavement strips crossing in the foreground. Source way `664532520` follows `(9.54,20.91) → (10.91,21.30) → (21.33,24.29)` in world X/Z. Way `1335178880` crosses it at node `12352271652`, `(10.91,21.30)`, and continues toward `(-0.32,60.64)`. Both OSM notes explicitly identify paths following yellow tactile paving. Their topology is supported by the source. Their inferred 2.5 m movement width is not evidence for isolated curbed pavement ribbons. Keep the walking paths; derive plaza/pavement surfaces and tactile strips from appropriate physical data. The phrase “movement/render corridors” in `network-contract.md:25` needs a precise limit so renderer integration cannot repeat this interpretation.

**F3 — P2: Mapped stop controls disappear before the vehicle consumer.** `tools/network/generate.ts:271` splits roads at `highway=stop`, but controller construction around `:200` considers crossings and traffic-signal nodes only. Neither `LaneEdge` nor `Junction` records stop priority. Concrete node `1496233582`, tagged `direction=forward`, belongs to way `30012066`; its retained lane sections and outgoing turns have null junction and signal group. Node `5133172807` on way `138594011` provides another case. The graph has 846 turn sections with no junction, so receiving-lane capacity checks alone cannot establish conflict arbitration at all intersections. Preserve mapped stop/priority information and a shared conflict identity for relevant uncontrolled movements, or document and implement an explicit conservative intersection admission contract before vehicle integration. This finding concerns information needed by the future consumer; it does not require actors to be implemented in Phase 6.

The signal state machine itself has a coherent entry-versus-clearance distinction. The occupied callback must describe committed actors; waiting pedestrians on a sidewalk must not hold a geometrically broad envelope. Future vehicle code must stop its front before entry, reserve receiving space, remember commitment across internal group changes, and track its tail after the centre leaves. There are 762 same-junction successor transitions whose group IDs differ because the new piece has a different heading. A consumer that calls `canEnter` again at each such transition can deadlock a committed vehicle on another approach's red. The contract's instruction to let committed actors clear is therefore essential and needs an integrated regression. This is a consumer obligation, not an additional reported controller defect.

Underground way exclusions and the separate OSM output are appropriate. The missing-ground intervals are reported, not hidden. Independent reverse traversal confirms 3,542 lane sections can reach an exit and confirms the two listed unusable entry IDs, but those measurements establish graph reachability only, not the correctness of its disconnections. The public decoder's explicit `Uint8Array` copy addresses the Buffer-view problem; the three byte-view tests passed. The HTTP tests exercised the separate mount, MIME types, traversal rejection and existing full-body Range behaviour without a browser.
