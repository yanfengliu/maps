**Reject this candidate as the intended realism milestone; request one ballast correction.** There is a small surface-detail gain: the previously flat deck/support faces have restrained variation, the sleepers remain distinct from the steel, and the rail lines stay readable. The principal new visible treatment, however, makes the track bed resemble fieldstone paving. More texture is not yet a convincing improvement in railway realism.

Cartographic has no supplied before frame, so its appearance is judged on its own. Exact paths and digests are in image-bindings.json.

1. **Introduced, material finding: ballast reads as polygon paving.** In the Satellite candidate's near left track bed, especially approximately x=0–480, y=470–720, large contiguous polygon cells have dark, nearly continuous joints and smooth interiors. The thin vertical bed edge carries the same stone-wall impression. At native size, this reads as a laid surface with grout lines rather than loose broken aggregate. Cartographic suppresses the contrast but retains the same cell pattern; it remains legible but looks like a pale paved slab. The baseline was overly plain, yet it did not assert this incorrect material. This is the only introduced issue that blocks my bounded approval.
2. **Inherited, larger realism limit: the portals remain cutouts in a low-detail building wall.** Satellite's broad dark blue facade and left wall are visibly blurred/smeared while the rails are sharp; the concrete-looking aperture returns are simple planar strips. In Cartographic, repeated glazing ends abruptly at those rectangular openings. These dominate the local scene's synthetic appearance. They exist in the baseline or concern unchanged source/geometry, so they are not a regression attributed to236. A material pass cannot establish structural plausibility or clearance.
