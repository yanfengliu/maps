## Grounded mechanism and falsifiable check

The frozen app invalidates TAA when city visibility changes (`src/app.ts:215-217`); post resets its accumulation index (`src/render/post.ts:268`). A permanently reused hidden-city image is not supported by that code. Direct and GTAO-disabled controls share different subsets, while the measured invalid scene target already precedes GTAO, bloom and final output. The original first-copy hypothesis is narrowed accordingly: TAA can retain/spread an invalid scene sample, but the source-pixel instrument shows it need not create it.

There is a concrete derivative-after-discard path. Three `meshphysical.glsl.js:165-186` executes clipping before normal and lighting calculations. `clipping_planes_fragment.glsl.js:53` conditionally discards. `normal_fragment_begin.glsl.js:6-12` uses either normalized position derivatives for flat shading or normalized interpolated normals; `lights_physical_fragment.glsl.js:5-10` differentiates the normal for roughness even on ordinary smooth Standard materials. The existing facade shader also evaluates `fwidth` after that discard (`src/scene/tile-materials.ts:177,239,280`). The [Khronos GLSL ES specification, jump statements](https://registry.khronos.org/OpenGL/specs/es/3.2/GLSL_ES_Specification_3.20.html#jump-statements) states that derivatives following nonuniform discard are undefined. Finite CPU attributes therefore do not guarantee finite shaded output at a clip silhouette. This is a grounded hypothesis, not a demonstrated first failing expression or a claim about every renderer.

Bloom explains the amplification without being the demonstrated origin. Its high-pass samples input radiance and mixes it (`LuminosityHighPassShader.js:54-62`), progressively blurs five mip levels (`UnrealBloomPass.js:315-334`, shader sums `:415-426`), combines them (`:476-481`) and adds the result back. None of these operations repairs nonfinite input. A small invalid region can therefore spread widely, consistent with the observed 7-to-920,895 growth.

**Rejected control:** the GPU owner preserved `aoi-deferred-discard-experiment.ts`, which chains the prior material hook and moves the same clipping fragment include to the end of the shader (`:16-29`). `deferred-discard-run/run.log` still records a scene-draw NaN (`0x7fff`) at `(574,604)` and the owner reports the final overview remains black. This control did not fix the failure and must not be accepted as a shader-order fix. Its three recorded center-ray hits are all outside the west plane; the first data491/batch51 hit is 1.096783 m outside. Those rays therefore do not identify the actual surviving fragment. The experiment supports retaining the localization while rejecting the proposed remedy; it does not prove which shader expression produces the NaN. The owner is restoring ordinary hard clipping.

The next bounded discriminator is the separately required cap validity repair with the same scene/pass readback. If invalid pixels persist, identify the actual surviving raster/sample material rather than extending the rejected shader-order guess. Any eventual positive result still needs multiple sides/cameras, both styles, moving/still TAA, normals/shadows and exact final boundary checks. Removing passes, cropping, suppressing bad RGB, changing source geometry or declaring screenshot failure are not acceptable fixes or proof.

## Separate cap-input defect

The new cap's intended sequenced material is plain MeshStandardMaterial, roughness 0.95, with computed positions/normals and no texture maps (`buildings-sequenced-diagnostic.ts:267-273`, frozen `cutout-sections.ts:113-119`). Missing UVs alone are not a defect for that plain material. Diagnosis-01's concurrent facade walk could patch new caps and attach procedural state; explicit sequencing removes that eligibility race from the newer probe. It does not remove the measured source-pixel NaN.

An independent CPU audit of the exact frozen `sectionRegion` and `sectionGeometry` on all 150 independently cached leaf slices produced 8,261 triangles, zero nonfinite positions/normals, but **5,622 zero-normal vertices on 1,874 collapsed triangles across 129 sections**. See `cap-normal-audit.mjs` and `cap-normal-audit.json`, including four exact witnesses and source/input hashes. The Float64 area test is `>1e-12` at `cutout-sections.ts:95-97`, before conversion to Float32 world coordinates and `computeVertexNormals` at `:117-118`. For example data458/batch116/north has a roughly 6.15 micrometre-wide slab whose first/third vertices become identical after conversion, yielding three zero normals.

This audit uses the independently preserved raw section inputs and tests before scene inverse transformation. It is not a new decode audit or a GPU rasterization proof. Collapsed triangles normally have no raster area, so they cannot simply be declared the seven-pixel cause; the identified bad source triangle is separate. The cap constructor nevertheless needs to emit valid quantized geometry/normals, with a check on its actual Float32 output. That bounded repair must retain source boundary endpoints and must not become general topology healing or tolerance expansion.
