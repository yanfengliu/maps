## Findings and disposition

**1006-V1 remains open, high — depth is now visible, but the facade reads as open dark compartments.** Candidate3 replaces the pale opaque panes and heavy grid with thin bright floor/corner lines around nearly black bays. Recess floors, ceilings, side returns and a few cream boxes/rectangles can now be distinguished, which is a substantive improvement in depth. Their strong compartment silhouette and sparse interior objects dominate while a continuous glazed outer surface is difficult to perceive. In `noon-close.png`, x330–725/y0–445, the rows read like exposed shelving or a cut-open building. The same result holds at lower-oblique x445–815/y0–430 and held-out x480–850/y0–425, noon and dusk. Both block views reduce the new facade to dark horizontal bands below the retained roof/ad, approximately x316–594/y575–705. The change remains visible at block scale but is not yet convincing material completion.

Dusk provides some observable specular response: the isolated bright point is near x450/y230 in `dusk-close.png` and x626/y298 in `dusk-lower.png`, with different broad tint between angles/times. That does not outweigh the open-compartment reading across the building. These static points are not classified as floating fixtures or leaks; their cause was not established by pixels alone. The requirement is perceptually convincing glass with depth, not merely a reflective highlight or a structurally present glass material.

**1006-V2 is only partially resolved; 1009-V2, medium — photo-window ghosts are removed but source lettering is fragmented.** The new dark sign backings have much cleaner edges and no visible old window grid crossing the new bays. That addresses the original compositing defect. However, `noon-close.png` x409–577/y379–402 shows STARBUCKS COFFEE reduced to disconnected blocky white strokes, with much of the character structure missing. It is less recognizable than candidate2's soft but readable lettering. The same fragmented result appears in `noon-lower.png` x474–590/y344–370 and in both dusk counterparts; held-out scale further reduces recognition. South TSUTAYA, approximately x541–641/y426–462 in close, remains recognizable but has coarse broken strokes. Preserving the source sign's character requires retaining enough of its full strokes to read, as well as removing surrounding photographic windows.

I observed no new broad halo, spur or obvious bleeding outside the plaque perimeters in these originals. The visible stroke loss cannot be attributed to the derivative-at-crop-edge caveat in 1008 from these images; mask/source resolution, cropping and sampling remain unseparated causes. This review therefore records a visual readability failure, not a shader-causality finding or cross-GPU portability result.
