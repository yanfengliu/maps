## 1. Crosswalk antialiasing loss: source path and runtime transition proved

Frozen candidate `src/agents/local-walk-prototype.ts:70` returns `moving: () => started && !refusal`. It stays true while turning, momentarily stationary, or handing back; it does not mean only root translation. Frozen `src/app.ts:248` combines `!localWalker?.moving()` with camera, tiles and population stillness. Main's existing population path has the same whole-scene policy at `src/app.ts:243`; the candidate adds the local actor to it.

`src/render/post.ts:246-248,263-268` copies that decision to the single scene-wide `TAARenderPass.accumulate` and clears its accumulated index. Run 4 confirms the actual change: frame 1578 has `taaAccumulating=true, taaSamples=32`; frame 1642 has `false,0`. The recorded camera values are identical. GTAO, exposure and bloom remain active with the same values. This explains why starting one actor removes accumulated antialiasing from stationary crossing stripes too.

The moving path is not completely un-antialiased: `post.ts:172-180` gives the composer and the scene sample target 4× MSAA. But `post.ts:184` sets `sampleLevel=0`. The frozen Three implementation in `TAARenderPass.js:84-89` calls its SSAA superclass when accumulation is false; `SSAARenderPass.js:172,281-284` selects a single `[0,0]` sample. Thus the concrete change is 32 temporal jitter positions to one current-frame spatial position plus 4× MSAA. The native stripe-quality change is observed; its exact contribution from geometry coverage versus material/shader detail was not isolated.

| Option | Cost and risk | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In `post.ts`, use `sampleLevel=1` while moving, `0` while accumulating. | Two current-frame jittered scene renders instead of one, using existing targets and no new dependency; additional scene/shadow work and copy draw, not a measured doubling of the whole frame. GTAO/bloom remain once per composer frame. | Smallest coherent first trial. Keep `accumulate=false` while the person moves. Compare actual native output and GPU frame cost before choosing it. |
| Add a moving-only spatial edge pass such as SMAA or FXAA. | Extra full-screen pass(es); FXAA can soften signs and thin paint, SMAA needs its lookup textures and extra plumbing. No temporal actor ghosting by design. | Fallback if two scene samples cost too much or fail to resolve stripes; effectiveness unmeasured here. |
| Ignore actor motion or accumulate only the static city separately. | Ignoring motion can freeze/ghost the actor. Separate city/actor composition needs correct depth, AO, shadows and occlusion, expanding architecture. | Do not use for this bounded repair. |

Verification for the proposed AA trial: repeat the same held-camera pre-Start and post-Start view at native resolution in both styles, retain movement telemetry, inspect thin crossing edges plus actor silhouette/signage, and review a short turn/stride sequence for ghosting. Compare frame cost on the same GPU. Do not claim that the proposed two samples were already observed, that MSAA is absent, or that whole-population performance is proved.

## 2. Weak shoe grounding: wiring exists; map scale is the strong suspect

The local walker already uses shared shadow and AO infrastructure. Frozen `src/agents/render/local-walk-draw.ts:38-42` creates deformed depth/distance/normal materials, assigns `customDepthMaterial` and `customDistanceMaterial`, sets `receiveShadow=true`, casts from the near LOD, and registers the normal material. Lines 53-56 feed the same sampled pose textures and root transform to those draws. The retained staging copy exactly matches freeze 264's draw-file digest. Main's generic human renderer uses the corresponding registration pattern at `src/agents/render/humans.ts:125-145`.

The shared receiver and light paths are present: `src/render/renderer.ts:37-38` enables PCF-soft shadows; `src/scene/roads.ts:62,72,78` enables road/pavement/paint receiving; `src/render/post.ts:188-201` installs agent-aware GTAO; `src/render/normal-pass.ts:21-38,52-59` renders registered deformed normal materials. **There is no missing near-actor cast-shadow flag to fix. No existing dedicated bounded per-foot/contact-shadow renderer was found in `src/`; the reusable pieces are these shared passes and the foot/support data below.**

The captured run explicitly navigates to `time=noon` (`artifacts/native-capture261-20260928/capture.mjs:67`, frozen runner digest verified). Therefore the dusk cutoff is not an explanation for these images. The observer did not record `lighting.state()`, so this is the admitted URL/source path, not a directly sampled shadow-enable value.

`src/scene/lighting.ts:33-34,86-94` uses a 2048 map over 1400 m: about **0.684 m per texel**. It applies **0.6 m normal bias**, and depth bias **-0.0006** over near 100/far 3200, equivalent to **-1.86 m in the light-depth range**. Three's shader adds the normal bias to the world receiver point and the depth bias to shadow-coordinate Z (`shadowmap_vertex.glsl.js:18-19`, `shadowmap_pars_fragment.glsl.js:124`). Those settings are city-scale and are a strong source-level explanation for poor human/sole shadows. Their actual contribution is a hypothesis until isolated in a rendered comparison. Changing a source parameter is not evidence that the contact improved.

GTAO is already receiving the posed person, but it uses a world radius of 1.4 m, 16 samples and blend 0.62 (`post.ts:73,85,192-201`). It may produce little stable darkening under shoes occupying only a few screen pixels. That explanation is also unisolated: no AO-only/normal/depth image was captured by this review.

## Reusable foot/support outputs and bounded grounding options

`LocalGaitSample` already carries one sampled `root`, `gait` and `input` (`src/agents/population/local-walk-gait.ts:22`). `sample.gait.feet[side]` carries normalized position `p`, `yaw`, `mode` and support `patch`; interpolation updates them together at lines 42-43. Do not drive presentation from the newer fixed-step root while the visible mesh uses an interpolated sample.

`src/agents/population/local-walk-support.ts:23-29` exposes `createLocalFloor(context).floorAt(worldX, worldZ)`, returning support Y and an explicit `hero:` patch with triangle IDs and seam status. `context.triangles` retains the actual support geometry and height law. `normalizedFloor` converts through the existing context origin and scale. The existing admission at lines 44-49 evaluates `engine.scenePose(...).point`, `rig.packet.soles[side]`, `rig.byId` and the same support triangles; these are usable source definitions, not a request to rerun its accepted numerical proof. A future presentation can reuse the existing GPU sampled positions/sole IDs rather than inventing a second animation clock or performing fresh full-body CPU work.

- **Bounded contact presentation:** add a small soft per-foot contact treatment to the local draw path, anchored to that same sampled foot/sole and its admitted support patch, fading with clearance during swing. Reuse support height/triangle identity; confine it to the near actor and dispose it with the actor. Expected cost is small added presentation geometry/shader work; slope/seam leakage, doubled GTAO darkening, floating blobs and airborne dark footprints are the material risks. This is a new presentation helper, not an existing renderer to turn on, and it does not replace a real directional cast shadow or physical-contact evidence.
- **Reuse the shared AO first for diagnosis:** an AO-only and normal/depth witness from the existing pass can establish whether the actual soles reach that buffer. Tune locally only if the observed defect supports it. Raising global AO strength/radius may dirty every facade and still miss subpixel contact, so it is not the recommended blanket fix.
- **Directional shadow quality:** a dedicated near shadow region/cascade could reuse the existing deformed caster materials, but adds another shadow pass and coverage policy. Increasing the global map from 2048 to 4096 costs roughly 4× shadow storage/raster footprint yet still gives 0.342 m texels. Global bias reduction risks acne throughout the city. Neither change is justified without wider scene evidence and neither is needed to reopen gait work.

Grounding verification should retain the close front/turn poses 2392/2401/2556, then inspect planted and raised feet in both styles at noon and dusk. Check that support seams and nearby paint receive no stray dark geometry. If a foot-contact approximation is chosen, name it as authored presentation. Root owns acceptance of both appearance fixes before the default milestone is called visually accepted.
