# 701 — Storefront native visual review

2026-10-11. **REJECT.** The pilot does not clearly improve visible occupation at block distance or provide readable depth close and obliquely. This verdict concerns the private candidate, not adoption of runtime700's evidence.

I inspected each of the eight original 1280×720 PNGs individually with `view_image` at `detail: original`, without a contact sheet, resizing, editing or recapture. I rehashed all eight; every SHA-256 matches runtime700. The paired cameras and preserved original building geometry are admitted from that independent runtime review, rather than independently re-tested here.

At noon block distance, the pilot replaces the photographic ground-floor surface with nearly uniform tan panes around a dark centre. At dusk block distance, the left pane becomes a bright cream rectangle and the right stays warm beige. Their increased brightness attracts attention but supplies no legible merchandise, furnishings, people, signage or layered interior that would read as occupation.

The close view makes the failure clearer: both lit bays remain blank surfaces, with hard rectangular terminations and bright peripheral edges. No back wall, floor or other spatial cue establishes depth. The oblique view still reads as flat luminous patches applied to the facade; changing viewpoint does not reveal a convincing room. The fundamental visual mistake is treating a brighter pane as evidence of an occupied storefront.

The middle bay remains dark as required. I see no obvious change to the surrounding crossing paint, pavement, trees, poles or adjacent buildings in these four pairs. No added label is visibly clipped, and there is no continuous glow ribbon; those absences do not satisfy the missing visual gain.

This static, local comparison does not prove all-map quality, movement, performance or pass-specific GPU shader compilation. No browser, server or persistent reader was launched; all file readers closed. No source changes or tuning were made.
