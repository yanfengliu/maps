Owner: `contact_contract_design`. Detached base: `927250018e86db4c89c9063a8f5f166ee15eb391`. Workspace: `artifacts/contact-contract-design/wt`. This is an authored design and two small CPU checks. No walking source, controller, production code, data, renderer or accepted support choice changed.

## Decision

The next useful experiment is a causal route replay with a read-only contact observer, followed only if needed by a separately reviewed foot-target extension. It must start at the actor's actual route birth and carry the controller state forward. Selecting a convenient pose at source 34 or 43.5 m would answer a different question.

The current frozen F27 controller can supply real causal foot states for a diagnostic. Its acceptance does not cover world contact: its moving branch bypasses foot admission, its landing height is body Y plus the reference ankle height, and its foot correction carries yaw without pitch or roll. Its reference loader supplies one commuter-male near rig. Renaming the existing callback to a world-contact adapter does not repair these gaps.

The nine-case experiment is not yet executable from the available accepted inputs. It needs an explicit conditional support assignment from source station zero through the local test exit, a supported local exit for each adjusted route, and matching reference data for office-male and commuter-female. These are concrete missing inputs. The first two do not require a new survey-grade reconstruction. Root may authorize conditional geometry inputs under Review 40 without declaring them the production source choice.

## The smallest candidate path experiment

The output is one immutable candidate per actor, or a named refusal. Preserve the source route itself. A separate local offset function may differ only inside a frozen repair interval `[a,b]`; it must equal the seeded preferred offset at both ends and outside the interval. Pin entry/exit XZ and heading, support assignment, full source-to-physical mapping, and the reason those endpoints are usable. A center hit alone does not finalize a usable endpoint: the incoming and outgoing actual planted feet must also pass the contact observer.

For the two seams, first keep the existing XZ track and test the actual foot motion across it. Do not alter the mesh or the offset just to make a center ray hit. Keep the opposed upper triangles and the real gap in the record. A foot that never plants across the slit needs its actual contact evidence; a planted foot that does span it needs witnesses on the relevant opposed banks. A body origin above the slit is not by itself a failure or success.

For the seven exterior cases, use one finite local graph once supported endpoints have been supplied. Its station set is the existing constructor's cuts at source-polyline derivative boundaries, its existing 0.25 m subdivisions, the fixed interval endpoints, and the exact observed failure stations. Its lateral candidates at each station are the preferred offset, zero, and each endpoint/midpoint of the selected upper triangles' cross-section intervals inside the original corridor. Candidate station/lateral positions are derived from actual geometry; no new lateral clearance threshold substitutes for the actor's geometry. Edges only connect consecutive source stations. Preserve monotone source order and use the shipped `sampleRoute` and `walkingCorridorContains` arithmetic.

Choose the shortest 3D candidate after selected-surface Y fitting, with total departure from the preferred offset and then deterministic station/lateral order as tie-breakers. Record every rejected edge and its cause. Bound this one experiment to 1,024 stations and 128 lateral candidates per station, and evaluate at most the first 16 complete candidates per actor. These are declared search-work bounds, not new contact tolerances or a claim of global completeness. A limit produces `bounded-search-exhausted`, not `unwalkable`. The canonical 0.25 m constructor chord spacing, 0.5 m join setting, corridor width, body dimensions, route choices and all contact targets stay unchanged.

The edge geometry check must retain holes. A strict centerline union test is useful for exterior paths, but cannot be the final seam predicate. A seam-crossing edge carries an unresolved finite-foot obligation until causal replay supplies its real stance. Keep collision square/rotating body checks separate from the foot observer. Checking rectangle edges alone is not a proof of the rectangle's interior in a nonconvex corridor.

The original constructor materializes explicit turns at heading changes. Use those actual events; do not replace them with a guessed gait phase or change their yaw-rate setting. A path that needs a body/foot turn model beyond the approved component is reported as unaccepted. A finite graph miss and a locomotion refusal have different result codes.

## Causal replay and the path/controller boundary

Use the exact F26 `initialWalkingState` and `advanceWalking` on the frozen candidate, and the real fixed-step clock. Initialize the controller once at the real route birth: source and physical travel are zero, speed is zero, body is at the candidate's source-zero pose, and walk phase is zero because the production distance channel is zero. Do not copy the ordinary renderer's later phase into the candidate. F27 owns its subsequent `sourcePhase`, release times, targets, plant anchors and blend; phase is not recomputed from source station on every tick. Preserve the existing spawn/variant/scale stream and report the chosen initial idle/reference convention separately from production VAT interpolation.

A minimum *conditional component* replay can use one disclosed deterministic speed command schedule from birth to beyond the local exit, with the existing cadence and speed-ramp rule and explicit hold/release controls. This produces a real candidate-controller trajectory, not the original population's trajectory. A population claim instead requires the live planner/admissions to run against the candidate's own same-tick source station and physical footprint. Replaying old population body positions, gate decisions or speed observations against a new offset path cannot establish that claim. Retain all 150 identities and the nine denominators in either mode. Freeze the selected mode and its command schedule before examining the seam; do not search phases or delays for a passing crossing.

For every fixed tick record actual start/end body XYZ/yaw, physical arc consumed, source station/occurrence, elapsed move/turn/hold time and authority inputs. F26 can spend part of one update translating and part turning and can cross multiple path steps. Feeding only its final pose and average speed to F27 would merge those histories. Add a read-only optional segment observation to the advancer, or expose its already computed ordered segments: `{fromTime,toTime,fromPose,toPose,physicalDelta,sourceFrom,sourceTo,mode,heldBy}`. An observer may not change arithmetic or committed output; observer-enabled and observer-free results must match exactly on the retained F26 controls.

The diagnostic must feed those actual boundaries to locomotion in chronological order and explicitly test the resulting update schedule. Fixed simulation time remains 60 Hz; event subintervals end on the fixed-tick boundary and sum to its `dt`. A final held remainder advances time with zero body/source/physical motion. Do not silently discard turn time, advance fake distance to animate a pivot, or hold the controller's clock while an admitted airborne foot still needs to land. Subinterval calls and turn-to-walk handback extend Review 50's finite domain; they need their own results even when controller bytes are unchanged.

The existing controller retains `turnControl`; sending positive speed after a turn still takes the stationary path and throws. Therefore a later coupling needs one explicit handback at a completed, admitted, aligned, both-planted state, preserving every foot anchor, derivative, phase and blend. It must also define what happens when a source hold freezes body yaw mid-turn: cancel the remaining yaw request, let only already admitted foot motion settle on held authority, and record failure if that cannot happen. Handback while a foot is airborne, resetting phase, and deleting state to evade a refusal are disqualifiers.

Represent the actual displayed scale by a single consistent transform. A first unchanged-controller diagnostic can normalize world translation and speed by that actor's scale, run its own rig in native asset metres, and transform all returned vertices and anchors back to world metres. Record that the inherited height/drop policy then scales with the actor. Compare world contact and drift with unchanged 15 mm and 5 mm bounds. Do not scale those tolerances. This diagnostic uses the controller's yaw-only basis and reports the actual surface normals; it grants no sloped-basis acceptance.

No new controller behavior is needed merely to observe its failure. A throw, posture refusal, inadmissible moving departure, missing reference data, unassigned source interval or contact violation yields the first exact row and stops that case as incomplete. Other cases may proceed without removing the refused one from the count.

## Concrete contact observer

Read the selected drawable GLB scene and triangle references through the same selected-part admission used by the renderer. Pin the model/VAT hashes, actual LOD, scale, drawn vertex IDs, shoe triangle groups, sole sample selection, reference joint palette and full skin weights. Use the native44 whole-mesh correspondence method for each required variant instead of substituting the one accepted rig. The initial experiment can explicitly request near geometry for every diagnostic actor; production near/medium/far selection and switches remain uncovered until each selected LOD is exercised.

Two quantities retain Review 40's proposed targets. For each planted interval, the contact-height residual is the signed distance of a real drawn sole point to a named selected support triangle at a projection inside that triangle. Report both normal and vertical residuals. At least one actual sole witness must be within 15 mm; this extends the old minimum-Y check and does not claim all sole vertices touch. Planted drift is the maximum Euclidean displacement in world metres of every retained sole sample from its own position at the committed plant event, until its actual release; it must be below 5 mm. The same points and committed baseline persist across path and triangle boundaries. A gap supplies no height value, and switching triangle IDs cannot reset drift.

Add a separate, explicitly proposed rigid-shoe support predicate to make the height test nonvacuous. Form contact patches by clipping the projected actual sole triangles against the assigned surface triangles and the 15 mm clearance band. Retain each patch's shoe triangle, support triangle, vertices and residual range. Their convex hull must have nonzero area and contain the vertical projection of the actual foot-joint load point, with the load-point assumption stated. This is a finite quasi-static rigid-shoe model; it is not a human centre-of-mass, force, friction, balance or compliant-shoe simulation. It may reject a legitimate toe/heel roll that this model does not describe. Such a rejection is not geometric impossibility of the route.

The hull may span an unsupported interior slit. Do not subtract the sole footprint from the surface union and require zero uncovered area. Instead retain the unsupported regions and the real contact patches. When a single planted shoe spans an internal seam, require positive-area contact patches on both actual opposed banks and preserve their IDs/heights; one edge extrapolated across empty space does not qualify. When the actual feet avoid planting over the seam, judge the observed support/release sequence instead of manufacturing a spanning pose. A rigid foot bridging a larger hole is at most success of this explicit rigid-shoe model, not proof of anatomical or visual plausibility.

Check whole-shoe clearance separately at every observed pose, including swing and underside/side triangles, against assigned support and nearby non-support geometry that could obstruct it. Triangle-pair XZ clipping followed by signed height extrema on the clipped vertices detects an interior surface penetration that corner rays miss; degenerate projected faces require edge/triangle intersection checks. Report signed penetration without converting the 15 mm contact tolerance into permission to sink the shoe. Float32 packing error must be separately measured; an uncertain sign within its bound is reported as uncertain, not welded away. Full-body checks include at least valid leg reach and the same actual full-mesh correspondence. They do not inherit shoe-clearance coverage for the rest of the body.

Sample the real state at every fixed-tick and actual release/apex/landing boundary and at five fractions of each recorded motion interval, matching the existing finite trajectory-check pattern. Record the full denominator and every early stop. These checks prove only those sampled poses and any explicitly bounded geometric segments. They do not prove continuous swept skinned-mesh collision safety. A future interval proof must bound the actual pose trajectory; F27's ankle polynomials alone do not bound the nonlinear leg solve and skinned vertices. Continuous safety remains unclaimed until that bound exists.

## Acceptance, controls and stop conditions

The finite experiment's success requires all nine named rows to reach and leave their declared local interval through causal replay; all original 150 identities and nine complete routes remain unchanged; all source assignments and real transition witnesses are explicit; fitted 3D arc agrees with independent length sums; F26 holds and physical footprints remain in force; and the actual variant/scale/near meshes pass the declared contact, drift, reach and clearance model at every recorded observation. Separate fields report `geometry`, `contactModel`, `sourceAuthority`, `nativeMotion`, and `productionReady`. Only the first two can be candidates for this experiment's acceptance. Missing or refused is never a passing row.

The controls must include: the unchanged exact nine refusals; the present moving-denial red control; a center ray over a narrow slit with valid opposed finite contacts; all-corners-supported geometry with a true interior hole; one-sided support whose hull fails the declared load point; an upper/lower wrong-sheet substitution; an absent-level substitution; a cap-only ground substitution; a real height step; a shoe interior penetration missed by vertices/corners; slipped planted samples over 5 mm; no actual contact within 15 mm; changed variant/scale/reference palette; and a phase/tick discontinuity. Retain the Review 40 wrong-layer way `1464521728`, genuine wider-gap cases and pivot counterexample as separate regressions, rather than treating the two tiny seams as representatives of every support issue.

Existing F26 plateau equality, hold/release, rotating hull and no-rewind controls, and F27 ordered landing/departure, denial, cancellation, interval and whole-mesh controls remain required for any implementation extension. A synthetic hold is a labelled control, not a changed gate in one of the nine route cases. A branch that does not call admission, a sample set that never reaches the seam, a zero-pose row, a transplanted tick-200 phase, or an all-flat replacement scene must go red.

Native moving inspection in both styles remains necessary after a viable contact candidate exists. It must inspect approach, actual support transitions, departure/landing separation, stops, and handback with the real meshes at native size from useful body and sole views. This design ran no visual surface and grants no naturalness, production shader, whole-city, continuous physical safety or performance verdict.
