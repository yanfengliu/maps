## Interface and authority

`src/world/network-data.ts` defines version 1. `npm run data:network` builds `data/network/network.json`; the Vite middleware serves it at `/network/network.json`. `loadNetwork()` validates before returning. This OSM derivative database remains separate from every PLATEAU mesh and is never included in the Vite bundle. Its source timestamp and three SHA-256 digests bind the OSM extract, terrain mesh and road mesh used to build it.

Every point is in the existing world frame: metres, Y up, X east and Z south, origin at the crossing. `LaneEdge` and `WalkEdge` have stable IDs, endpoint node IDs, sampled points, arc length, movement width, source way ID, directed successor IDs and optional junction/group IDs. Vehicle edges also hold speed, same-direction lateral neighbours, `entryRule` and mapped `sourceControlNodeIds`. Lateral links are reciprocal, outside every conflict area, and share at least 8 m of same-direction parallel overlap. `laneChangeOverlap(a,b)` returns the permitted arc intervals on both edges; the consumer may change lanes only within those intervals. A missing neighbour is not permission to cross a junction or opposing carriageway. Walking paths are directed pairs, with `:f` and `:r` suffixes. The two populations plan routes through their own `nextIds`; shared coordinates never splice their graphs together.

Physical rendering reads `NetworkData.physical`, not movement-edge kinds or directed IDs. `physical.crossings` has one record per mapped source way, plus the explicitly authored scramble diagonal, with sampled `paths`, source ID, markings (`zebra`, `none`, `unknown`, `other`), raw marking tag, control (`signals`, `uncontrolled`, `unknown`), width and width provenance. Split or reverse movement edges do not create extra physical records. `none` forbids paint; `unknown` is not evidence of zebra markings. Source ways `1335178883`, `977916824` and `1335178868` explicitly retain no-markings metadata.

`physical.tactilePaths` retains mapped path geometry and its source reason. `extent: path` comes from a source note saying the path follows yellow tactile paving; a crossing's `tactile_paving=yes` records `endpoints`, and a non-crossing tag alone remains `unspecified`. Ways `664532520` and `1335178880` share source node `12352271652`: their movement paths survive, while their tactile source notes do not authorize wide raised pavement. Actual pavement surface geometry comes from the separate PLATEAU renderer extraction.

`physical.trafficControls` has one record per mapped source node, with original tags, world position, `kind` (`traffic_signals`, `stop`, `give_way`), source direction, a travel vector where approaches agree, source way IDs, concrete approach/entry-edge IDs and controller ID. Entry IDs trace actual graph predecessors to the union boundary when a mapped node lies inside a merged controller. Unknown direction stays explicit; a consumer must disclose any authored presentation orientation. A traffic-signal node does not establish the number or shape of physical heads. Inferred reservation controllers do not authorize poles, lights or road markings.

`Junction.controlKind` distinguishes `signal` from `reservation`; `controlSource` is `mapped`, `authored` or `inferred`. `SignalController` handles only signal junctions, and now lives inside the central `JunctionAdmissions` authority. Its `canEnter(groupId)` is phase eligibility only; the simulation must obtain central admission rather than using that method alone. `signalSnapshot()` reports stage, active group, amber group, elapsed/remaining seconds, clearance hold and cycle from that same controller. Amber allows no new entries. Actors that entered retain authority while clearing on red. A null group is not permission to enter a reservation. Style changes have no signal or network state.

`JunctionAdmissions` in `src/network/admissions.ts` is the sole admission owner for both populations and both controller kinds. `src/network/reservations.ts` re-exports it as `JunctionReservations` for import compatibility; the old footprint-only release API is gone. Call `resolve(fixedDt, requests)` once per fixed tick, after observing the preceding positions and before moving either population. It advances its signals and resolves the complete request batch, returning explicit granted actor IDs. A reservation compound admits one actor of either kind. A signal compound initially admits one vehicle at a time, or multiple pedestrians during its exclusive pedestrian phase. Even if two crossing/turn paths share an approach sector, they cannot receive simultaneous vehicle commitments. This is a conservative admission policy, not evidence that 200 vehicles have acceptable throughput.

Create a `RoutePassage` with `createRoutePassage(network, kind, completeRouteEdgeIds, entryIndex)` in `src/network/passages.ts`. Use the actor's actual complete planned route and retain this object throughout its commitment; a fabricated object or another network snapshot is rejected. Directed adjacency is checked. Repeated edge IDs are legal, with occurrence indices distinguishing later laps. `lastConflictIndex` is the last occurrence of this compound before the next distinct authority. The route must include an outside exit section after that occurrence. A consumer must not supply a truncated prefix, change its route while committed, or report a later occurrence before physically traversing it. This factory validates the supplied plan; it does not infer an unstated destination.

Each `AdmissionRequest` supplies stable `actorId`, `kind`, `entryEdgeId`, `passage`, optional `routeIndex` (defaulting to the entry occurrence), actual collision `footprint`, continuously measured `stoppedSeconds`, actual `yieldSatisfied` and `receivingSpace`. Footprint position is the collision rectangle's centre, including any offset of the generated bounds from the rendered origin; it is not necessarily the vehicle model's origin. Its dimensions are the actual scaled collision envelope. Initial receiving-space eligibility must reserve enough exit capacity for the whole compound passage, before entering it. An entered actor retains that reservation and may clear internal edges even when the signal group changes. The later simulation must implement and measure this capacity reservation; this controller does not implement downstream lane queues.

`RoutePassage.controls` locates mapped stop/yield obligations using `routeIndex`, `distanceM` along that edge, source `nodeIds` and `rule`. Mapped positions project onto their retained directed approach paths; outer `physical.entryEdgeIds` remain admission boundaries and do not relocate an internal stop. A synthetic fixture without physical records uses entry-rule boundaries. Source node `1519695413` is 3.8552385707 m into section 2 of way `217934322`, after admission at section 0. This location is derived from the mapped node and inferred lane offset, not a surveyed painted stop line. Before passing a control, request measured eligibility for that route occurrence. Stop requires the authored 1-second continuous dwell and a measured traffic gap; yield requires the gap. A later lap requires fresh dwell. An observation that advances the footprint beyond an uncleared mapped control is rejected. The consumer measures dwell at this location and conflicting traffic eligibility; it must not supply hardcoded successful values.

Priority requests win within a two-second eligible-age bucket; older eligible buckets eventually outrank fresh priority arrivals, with stable actor ID as the last tie breaker. Reversed request order gives the same result. Ineligible requests do not accumulate age. `observe(actorId, {routeIndex, distanceM, footprint})` records monotonic actual progress. `release(junctionId, actorId, observation)` is the owner-checked equivalent. Both retain the commitment through internal gaps, changed edge/group IDs, internal stops and repeated route occurrences. Release requires progress beyond the planned last conflict occurrence and exact full-rectangle clearance of every original disk. `snapshot()` returns copied commitment observations; `occupied(id)` includes only entered commitments. An unused green grant expires when green ends, and `cancelPending` cannot cancel an entered actor.

Approach, physical conflict and exit portions of a lane are separate edges, split analytically at original conflict boundaries. Only physical conflict portions have `junctionId`. A null edge between two primitives of one compound can still be inside an actor's committed passage. A stopped outside queue does not occupy the controller; an entered actor in a physically empty internal gap does. Pedestrian staging remains on sidewalk edges, even when a conservative disk covers that sidewalk. The simulation must keep actors in their corridors and report actual route progress and footprints. These are consumer obligations; admission tests do not establish populated movement.

Overlapping areas and nearby legal route authorities share a compound controller. `Junction.conflictAreas` remains the exact set of original disks. `radiusM` only bounds them for display; merging never uses that enlarged circle as physical conflict geometry. `memberIds` preserves unique ownership of the preceding controllers. Offline grouping reaches a fixed point across both complete successor graphs: group consecutive authorities when their shortest outside route gap is below 12 m or any original-disk separation is below 11.6 m. Unioning can expose another close primitive pair, so a single pass is insufficient. Re-run after every union until no new grouping occurs.

`NetworkData.admissionBounds` and `src/network/admission-bounds.ts` pin an authored engineering envelope: scaled collision diagonal at most 11.1 m, plus 0.5 m stop gap, with the 12 m route grouping threshold. `assertSupportedFootprint(size, scale)` rejects invalid or larger actual envelopes. The generated bus collision body is 10.565 m by 3.28 m, diagonal 11.06244209 m; smaller generated classes fit as well. Runtime class dimensions must come from the generated vehicle manifest, not duplicated nominal body dimensions. A circumscribed radius of half the diagonal bounds every rectangle orientation; disjoint primitive surfaces separated by at least the full supported diagonal plus stop gap cannot simultaneously contain that body. `occupiesJunction` still uses exact oriented-rectangle/disk contact, rather than the old capsule that counted width again at the ends. This is a controller-clearance envelope, not proof that a bus fits every lane or swept turn.

## What comes from data and what is authored

The offline pipeline uses the existing scene meshes as its height instrument. It samples their triangles and resamples paths at no more than 1.5 m horizontally. The road builder's existing lift is retained; no scene mesh is edited. Missing terrain is missing, with the affected path intervals excluded and reported. The decoder now copies byte views explicitly, including Node Buffer; its old Buffer-specific corruption was reproduced before correction.

OSM supplies topology, lane tags, direction tags, access restrictions, sidewalks, crossing paths and surface traffic-signal nodes. Tagged elements win over the bare duplicate records emitted later by Overpass. Underground, elevated, indoor and inaccessible ways are filtered before adjacency. In particular, platform ways `664527469` and `664527472` must not join the surface scramble through shared node `291758776`.

Road lane count defaults to one each way, or one in the tagged one-way direction. Lane width defaults to 3 m, with tagged width divided across the lane count. Actual source tags include four consistent directional lane splits and two explicit widths. Width 1 m on way `136587664` is excluded from vehicle routes rather than widened. Contradictory lane totals and unsupported reversible direction tags fail with a named source error. Bidirectional `lanes=1` requires a future shared narrow-road model and is not silently turned into two lanes; none occurs in this source extract. Left-hand offsets, cubic turn connectors and lane ordering are approximations. They are not surveyed lane boundaries.

The pedestrian network uses mapped surface footway, pedestrian, path, steps and living-street geometry. It does not fabricate a sidewalk alongside every road. Missing corridor widths default to 2.5 m for walking paths, 5 m for pedestrian streets, 3 m for crossings and 5 m for scramble crossings. These are inferred movement extents around OSM centrelines, not measured sidewalk or pavement surface extents. Physical crossing paths are deduplicated from the same source path and height samples used for movement. Their separate marking facts decide whether paint is justified.

OSM way `754454449` supplies the mapped NW-SE diagonal. The authored NE-SW diagonal joins real surface sidewalk endpoints `3608568703` and `3608568744`, uses `sourceWayId: null`, and is listed in the authored-edge diagnostics. Both directions use the scramble controller. The visible crosswalk treatment must distinguish this authored completion from OSM source geometry in provenance.

The conflict extent is authored from the OSM crossing geometry, with a 39 m main scramble envelope and smaller crossing envelopes. A mapped surface signal without a covered crossing receives a conservative 12 m envelope. Uncovered junction nodes with at least three road arms receive compact reservation areas, at least 4 m in radius and large enough to contain their lane endpoints; uncovered mapped stop/yield nodes receive 4 m areas. These are not PLATEAU intersection polygons and are not a traffic survey. The builder includes all 78 qualifying surface traffic-signal nodes and 41 stop nodes, checking controller and applicable-entry coverage after merging. The simulation timings are also authored: no real Tokyo signal schedule is claimed.

Each signal approach sector has exclusive vehicle green. At the scramble, four approaches receive 16 seconds green, 3 seconds amber and at least 3 seconds empty clearance each. First pedestrian admission is at simulated second 88 in an empty run. Pedestrian entry lasts 25 seconds, followed by at least 3 seconds empty clearance; the next vehicle phase starts at second 116. Occupied clearance extends this schedule until entire committed passages clear. Other signal junctions use 12-second vehicle green and 16-second pedestrian entry. No compound has more than four authored approach sectors, and the longest empty cycle remains 116 seconds. Grouping preserves the maximum member timings and uses the four world-heading sectors; central vehicle serialization prevents incompatible simultaneous vehicle admissions across their distinct primitives.

Node-via `only_*` and `no_*` turn restrictions are applied before continuation decisions. Seven via-way, conditional or otherwise unsupported restrictions conservatively block their related entry rather than becoming permission. Their relation IDs and reasons remain in the output. Turn-only lane labels describe destination movements: same-way internal sections remain connected, and labels carry through a degree-two cross-way continuation until an actual choice. Explicit turn restrictions remain authoritative across these continuations. This fixes the rejected builder's broken forward lanes on `1071659161`/`1087233628` and reverse lanes on `1377702569`. Free-form source notes remain in source-control metadata; this is not a full interpreter of conditional Japanese traffic law.
