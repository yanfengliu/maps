## Interface and authority

`src/world/network-data.ts` defines version 1. `npm run data:network` builds `data/network/network.json`; the Vite middleware serves it at `/network/network.json`. `loadNetwork()` validates before returning. This OSM derivative database remains separate from every PLATEAU mesh and is never included in the Vite bundle. Its source timestamp and three SHA-256 digests bind the OSM extract, terrain mesh and road mesh used to build it.

Every point is in the existing world frame: metres, Y up, X east and Z south, origin at the crossing. `LaneEdge` and `WalkEdge` have stable IDs, endpoint node IDs, sampled points, arc length, movement width, source way ID, directed successor IDs and optional junction/group IDs. Vehicle edges also hold speed, same-direction lateral neighbours, `entryRule` and mapped `sourceControlNodeIds`. Lateral links are reciprocal, outside every conflict area, and share at least 8 m of same-direction parallel overlap. `laneChangeOverlap(a,b)` returns the permitted arc intervals on both edges; the consumer may change lanes only within those intervals. A missing neighbour is not permission to cross a junction or opposing carriageway. Walking paths are directed pairs, with `:f` and `:r` suffixes. The two populations plan routes through their own `nextIds`; shared coordinates never splice their graphs together.

Physical rendering reads `NetworkData.physical`, not movement-edge kinds or directed IDs. `physical.crossings` has one record per mapped source way, plus the explicitly authored scramble diagonal, with sampled `paths`, source ID, markings (`zebra`, `none`, `unknown`, `other`), raw marking tag, control (`signals`, `uncontrolled`, `unknown`), width and width provenance. Split or reverse movement edges do not create extra physical records. `none` forbids paint; `unknown` is not evidence of zebra markings. Source ways `1335178883`, `977916824` and `1335178868` explicitly retain no-markings metadata.

`physical.tactilePaths` retains mapped path geometry and its source reason. `extent: path` comes from a source note saying the path follows yellow tactile paving; a crossing's `tactile_paving=yes` records `endpoints`, and a non-crossing tag alone remains `unspecified`. Ways `664532520` and `1335178880` share source node `12352271652`: their movement paths survive, while their tactile source notes do not authorize wide raised pavement. Actual pavement surface geometry comes from the separate PLATEAU renderer extraction.

`physical.trafficControls` has one record per mapped source node, with original tags, world position, `kind` (`traffic_signals`, `stop`, `give_way`), source direction, a travel vector where approaches agree, source way IDs, concrete approach/entry-edge IDs and controller ID. Entry IDs trace actual graph predecessors to the union boundary when a mapped node lies inside a merged controller. Unknown direction stays explicit; a consumer must disclose any authored presentation orientation. A traffic-signal node does not establish the number or shape of physical heads. Inferred reservation controllers do not authorize poles, lights or road markings.

`Junction.controlKind` distinguishes `signal` from `reservation`; `controlSource` is `mapped`, `authored` or `inferred`. `SignalController` handles only signal junctions. The simulation calls `advance(stepSeconds, occupied)` once per fixed tick before either population updates. It rejects a variable or invalid step. `canEnter(groupId)` gates signal entry; `snapshot()` reports each signal junction's stage, active group, amber group, elapsed/remaining seconds, clearance hold and cycle. Amber allows no new entries. Actors that already entered continue clearing on red. A null group says nothing about reservation admission. Style changes have no signal or network state.

`JunctionReservations` in `src/network/reservations.ts` gives vehicles and pedestrians one exclusive lease per unsignalized conflict union. Call `resolve(fixedDt, requests)` once per fixed tick with the complete request batch. Each request supplies stable `actorId`, `kind`, `entryEdgeId`, continuously measured `stoppedSeconds`, actual `yieldSatisfied` and `receivingSpace`; the result explicitly lists granted actor IDs. Mapped stop requires the authored 1-second dwell and a measured traffic gap. Yield and pedestrian entry require the measured gap. Priority requests win within a two-second eligible-age bucket; older eligible buckets eventually outrank fresh priority arrivals, with stable actor ID as the last tie breaker. Reversing request iteration order does not change admission. Ineligible requests do not accumulate waiting age. The consumer must compute the conflicting approaches and receiving capacity; it must not hardcode a gap as available.

`release(junctionId, actorId, footprint)` observes the committed actor every tick. It retains a newly granted lease before entry, marks it entered when its footprint intersects the union, and releases only after the whole footprint has left. Internal edge transitions retain the lease and do not restart stop dwell or admission. The footprint includes position, heading, length and width; a vehicle centre outside is insufficient when its tail remains inside. `snapshot()` returns copied lease observations. The exclusive-union policy is deliberately conservative and its capacity has not yet been measured at population scale. Reservation junctions retain unused timing/group fields for the internal common shape, but produce no signal phases or hardware.

Approach, conflict and exit portions of a lane are separate edges, split analytically at the conflict boundary. Only the conflict portion has `junctionId`; a queue outside does not occupy it. A vehicle's tail can remain inside after its centre reaches an exit edge, so occupancy uses the full committed footprint through `occupiesJunction`, not only the current edge label. Pedestrian staging remains on sidewalk edges with `junctionId: null`, even if the conservative geometric envelope covers that sidewalk. The simulation must count only committed actors, keep pedestrians within their walking corridor, reserve receiving lane space before vehicle entry, and continue tracking clearance across edge transitions. These are consumer obligations; the signal unit tests do not establish that the simulation obeys them.

Overlapping conflict areas share one controller. `Junction.conflictAreas`, when present, is the exact union of the original disks. `radiusM` then bounds that union for display only; it is not an expanded conflict surface. Sampling, entry splitting and occupancy use the union. Independent controllers may not have overlapping physical areas. Merging never feeds an enclosing circle back into the overlap search.

## What comes from data and what is authored

The offline pipeline uses the existing scene meshes as its height instrument. It samples their triangles and resamples paths at no more than 1.5 m horizontally. The road builder's existing lift is retained; no scene mesh is edited. Missing terrain is missing, with the affected path intervals excluded and reported. The decoder now copies byte views explicitly, including Node Buffer; its old Buffer-specific corruption was reproduced before correction.

OSM supplies topology, lane tags, direction tags, access restrictions, sidewalks, crossing paths and surface traffic-signal nodes. Tagged elements win over the bare duplicate records emitted later by Overpass. Underground, elevated, indoor and inaccessible ways are filtered before adjacency. In particular, platform ways `664527469` and `664527472` must not join the surface scramble through shared node `291758776`.

Road lane count defaults to one each way, or one in the tagged one-way direction. Lane width defaults to 3 m, with tagged width divided across the lane count. Actual source tags include four consistent directional lane splits and two explicit widths. Width 1 m on way `136587664` is excluded from vehicle routes rather than widened. Contradictory lane totals and unsupported reversible direction tags fail with a named source error. Bidirectional `lanes=1` requires a future shared narrow-road model and is not silently turned into two lanes; none occurs in this source extract. Left-hand offsets, cubic turn connectors and lane ordering are approximations. They are not surveyed lane boundaries.

The pedestrian network uses mapped surface footway, pedestrian, path, steps and living-street geometry. It does not fabricate a sidewalk alongside every road. Missing corridor widths default to 2.5 m for walking paths, 5 m for pedestrian streets, 3 m for crossings and 5 m for scramble crossings. These are inferred movement extents around OSM centrelines, not measured sidewalk or pavement surface extents. Physical crossing paths are deduplicated from the same source path and height samples used for movement. Their separate marking facts decide whether paint is justified.

OSM way `754454449` supplies the mapped NW-SE diagonal. The authored NE-SW diagonal joins real surface sidewalk endpoints `3608568703` and `3608568744`, uses `sourceWayId: null`, and is listed in the authored-edge diagnostics. Both directions use the scramble controller. The visible crosswalk treatment must distinguish this authored completion from OSM source geometry in provenance.

The conflict extent is authored from the OSM crossing geometry, with a 39 m main scramble envelope and smaller crossing envelopes. A mapped surface signal without a covered crossing receives a conservative 12 m envelope. Uncovered junction nodes with at least three road arms receive compact reservation areas, at least 4 m in radius and large enough to contain their lane endpoints; uncovered mapped stop/yield nodes receive 4 m areas. These are not PLATEAU intersection polygons and are not a traffic survey. The builder includes all 78 qualifying surface traffic-signal nodes and 41 stop nodes, checking controller and applicable-entry coverage after merging. The simulation timings are also authored: no real Tokyo signal schedule is claimed.

Each signal approach sector has exclusive vehicle green. At the scramble, four approaches receive 16 seconds green, 3 seconds amber and at least 3 seconds empty clearance each. First pedestrian admission is at simulated second 88 in an empty run. Pedestrian entry lasts 25 seconds, followed by at least 3 seconds empty clearance; the next vehicle phase starts at second 116. Occupied clearance extends this schedule until committed actors leave. Other signal junctions use 12-second vehicle green and 16-second pedestrian entry. The largest current union has six original disks; the largest display bound is 63.112 m, no signal junction has more than four approach groups, and the longest empty cycle is 116 seconds.

Node-via `only_*` and `no_*` turn restrictions are applied before continuation decisions. Seven via-way, conditional or otherwise unsupported restrictions conservatively block their related entry rather than becoming permission. Their relation IDs and reasons remain in the output. Turn-only lane labels describe destination movements: same-way internal sections remain connected, and labels carry through a degree-two cross-way continuation until an actual choice. Explicit turn restrictions remain authoritative across these continuations. This fixes the rejected builder's broken forward lanes on `1071659161`/`1087233628` and reverse lanes on `1377702569`. Free-form source notes remain in source-control metadata; this is not a full interpreter of conditional Japanese traffic law.

## Verification bounds and handoff

`npm run data:network` validates the complete current extract, finite local coordinates, sampled arc lengths, endpoint adjacency, successor references, reciprocal outside-conflict lateral overlap, admission authority, mapped physical/source IDs, deduplication, applicable control entries, non-overlapping controllers, source digests, full AOI reach, source control coverage, both diagonals and the shared subway-node counterexample. It writes output only after validation. Repeated builds produce identical bytes: 10,217,404 bytes, SHA-256 `8ae12262cf19e4f1310ba9303ad9dbd6769b73d5c4fab1907bb006385350ab6c`.

The six scoped files `test/{network,network-review,network-mesh,network-serving,signals,signals-reservations}.test.ts` pass 41 tests. Their bounds include fourteen promoted OSM ways and original nodes/tags; synthetic lane/direction/restriction/ground-hole fixtures; turn permission carried to a true branch; mapped unmarked/tactile/stop metadata; deterministic reversed-batch reservation admission, 1-second stop dwell, actual yield eligibility, receiving capacity, opposite actor kinds, full-tail lease retention, malformed input and eligible-request service within a 5-second fixture; four synthetic signal cycles at 60 Hz; and exact Node/browser byte semantics. One headless HTTP run covers the network mount, asset MIME types, traversal refusal and existing full-body Range behaviour. Its ephemeral server and temporary directory close in cleanup. No browser or GUI was launched by this worker.

The complete generated graph was also driven through 360 simulated seconds at 60 Hz in empty and delayed-clearance runs. Every one of the 46 signal controllers completed at least three cycles. Empty scramble admission/cycle were 88/116 seconds. Holding its first clearance until second 27 delayed them to 92.9833/120.9833 seconds; the fixed-tick boundary explains the final 1/60-second fraction. Reservation controllers produced no signal snapshots. This exercise validates the controllers against full source geometry, not populated traffic behaviour.

The exact rejected builder was rerun in an isolated ignored copy against the new source gates: F0 internal and cross-way continuations, F1 conflict lateral links, and F2/F3 missing physical/control metadata all failed. The real dead-end exclusion remained a passing control. Separate isolated mutations removed cross-way permission propagation (the forbidden straight movement became allowed), skipped stop dwell (actor `s` was wrongly granted entry), and reduced footprint clearance to the actor centre (a vehicle lease released while its tail remained inside). Each named test failed for that semantic reason. Raw logs and candidate hashes remain under ignored `artifacts/network/` for focused re-review; the coordinator owns their permanent learning/devlog promotion.

The mesh regression was first run against the old decoder: browser and offset Uint8Array cases passed, while Buffer decoded the header/backing slab as positions, with first float `7.185598589700907e+22` instead of `0`. The corrected decoder uses `new Uint8Array(bytes.subarray(offset)).buffer`, and all three byte-view cases pass. The integration owner records this red proof in canonical learning/devlog documents.

These checks do not prove visual lane placement, correct street furniture, vehicle/pedestrian collision avoidance, receiving-lane capacity, first-person traffic behaviour or performance at 3,000/200 actors. Those belong to the integrated simulation/render acceptance and independent review. This document must not mark those later phases complete.
