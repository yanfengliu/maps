The [University Architect's Hoover photograph](https://architectplanning.stanford.edu/hoover-tower) shows a plain light shaft, tall arched crown openings and a red domed roof. These observations produce a subdued golden sandstone and terracotta family for historic buildings, pink stone with modeled ivy for Roble, and cooler light roofs and muted glazing for modern buildings. Original `color` and `roofColor` values remain unchanged; derived `detailWallColor` and `detailRoofColor` drive presentation. Procedural stone courses and tile seams fade with screen size to avoid distant shimmer. The material families are authored approximations, not measured reflectance or a campus-wide material survey.

Roble's entrance relief is anchored at original footprint vertex 34, OSM entrance node `6461239753`; its columns, balcony, divided doors and ivy follow the inspected photo but use estimated dimensions. Connected ivy is limited to the source northeast-facing front elevations; other elevations retain modeled pink stone. Bing's three projected stone arch surrounds span its already reviewed continuous west facade. Hoover gains bounded crown arches and cornice relief on its 75 m mapped part. The existing church exterior and its current-form seven upper openings and three entrance portals remain intact. These refinements stay within each building's original vertical envelope; facade relief projects by at most 0.34 m for the Roble balcony.

The [Stanford palm reference](https://trees.stanford.edu/ENCYC/PHOca.htm) describes Canary Island date palms with stout trunks, dense crowns and arching fronds, including the Palm Drive approach and its branching paths beside the Oval. This supports the distinct feathered palm silhouette visible in the inspected church and Bing photos. Broadleaf crowns and needleleaved conifers are deliberately different modeled shapes. Unknown species are modeled as broadleaf rather than asserted to be a specific tree. No tree positions are generated from this prose or a random planting grid.

## Rendering and acceptance

The local environment input is fetched and validated inside the existing dedicated geometry worker only when detail is requested at zoom 14 or closer with the camera center within 6 km of campus. Zooming into another Bay Area city does not start a fresh Stanford worker. Regional startup acquires no environment data, tree buffers or extra shader program. Failed network, HTTP, JSON and schema inputs name `/data/stanford-environment.geojson` and use the existing detail Retry path while the regional map remains usable. Context restoration reuses the complete cached mesh and regenerates GPU buffers through the existing layer lifecycle.

Trees, lamps and benches share the existing roof buffer and shader; no additional draw call, texture, per-tree object or animation loop is added. Environment vertices use building sentinel `-2`, distinct from the default unselected index `-1`, and dedicated solid kinds that cannot acquire facade windows, tile texture or building selection tint. Broadleaf crowns use interpolated normals; palm fronds and conifer tiers supply different silhouettes. All source points render from zoom 14; facade opening detail remains subject to the existing zoom/distance control. There is no screen-distance deletion of individual mapped trees.

Planar flat and skillion roofs now use their original triangulation without redundant subdivisions. Independent area and courtyard tests preserve their coverage and source height. The combined candidate contains 148,465 triangles and 23,160,540 GPU-buffer bytes, below the existing 150,000-triangle and 24 MiB limits, in two batched draws. Counts describe the complete fixed mesh; they do not establish 60 physical display frames per second on every device.

Uploads now perform one buffer allocation or at most 1 MiB of data transfer per render callback. Readiness and both custom draws wait for every byte of both buffers. Removal, context loss and transfer failure release partial allocations; restoration starts their upload again from byte zero. `uploadMs` measures accumulated CPU time spent in allocation/transfer calls, `uploadWallMs` includes the gaps between those callbacks, and `uploadSlices` counts the callbacks. Whole-buffer allocation is isolated and measured; a 1 MiB transfer cap is not a hardware-independent bound on driver allocation time.

The approved correction limits connected ivy to the photographed front-facing elevations and splits the transfers. Its focused final gate passed 17 assertions with 14 inspected images. The baseline remains under `artifacts/campus-environment/verification/`; corrected evidence is under its `final/` directory. Neither set was overwritten. Image and source manifests bind the inspected bytes.
