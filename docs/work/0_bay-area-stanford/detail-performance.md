# Stanford detail and frame-rate target

Owner: root coordinates integration, real-control acceptance and GPU measurements; startup_fix owns derived building geometry/rendering and its tests. Base code is `a012c04`, with startup verification on main at `38eb59a`. User request on 2026-10-20: fast loading, 60 fps, and substantially more detailed/refined buildings while keeping the Apple Maps-inspired style and colors.

Acceptance: preserve fast regional startup and only Stanford 3D; visibly improve roof silhouettes, eaves and facade depth at campus/detail zooms using sandstone, terracotta and muted accents; keep real footprint extents and courtyard holes, distinguish estimated details from source facts, preserve original source heights/provenance and existing search/selection/retry. Detail geometry must be deterministic, bounded and lazily prepared, with a few batched draws and complete GPU cleanup. The 60 fps target is measured during actual camera motion at 1920×1080 on the available GPU, with frame interval distribution and loading/steady-state bounds reported rather than a hardware-independent promise.

Contract: `prepareBuildingDetails` makes a cheap enriched copy containing derived wall height without changing source height. `createBuildingDetailsLayer` returns a MapLibre custom 3D layer with read-only stats and a selection setter. The regular extrusions retain source IDs and picking; the custom layer supplies roofs and facade details. Earcut is admitted as a small direct dependency for robust triangulation with courtyard holes. Do not alter the pinned source GeoJSON.

Verification: required build/types/unit/audit gates, actual footprint geometry and triangle-budget tests, native before/after campus and landmark views at multiple bearings/distances, desktop/mobile region and campus flows, failure/retry and layer lifecycle, and headless GPU render-event/frame-cadence measurement using actual controls. Keep raw output ignored. Independent read-only review inspects the final integrated changes.

Geometry is created in a dedicated worker at zoom 14 or closer, cached for style reconstruction and transferred to two GPU buffers. Facades appear from zoom 15.5 and fade with distance. The regional view creates no architectural geometry/worker. Final mesh: 100,514 triangles, 15,680,184 vertex bytes, at most two draws, below the 150,000-triangle guard. Worker preparation took 156.5–157 ms in final browser runs. The production worker asset is 12.75 kB; the main gzip is 305.11 kB versus 301.35 kB before detail. The existing MapLibre large-chunk warning remains.

An initial implementation synchronously waited for shader completion during the first campus frame, taking 45.2 ms of main-thread setup. The corrected version starts shader preparation at regional load and polls `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` without waiting for status early; an idle-time fallback is unit-checked for implementations without the extension. Final observed buffer/setup time was 5 ms during cold development flight and 4.3 ms in production preview. Parallel compile wall time (11.7/37.7 ms) is separate from main-thread blocking. Worker, pending compilation and GPU resources are released on removal, failure and page cleanup.

Measured with headless Chromium, ANGLE Direct3D11 on an NVIDIA RTX 4090, 1920×1080 drawing buffer, pixel ratio 1. Real Alt+arrow input continuously rotates the camera; the read-only observer records MapLibre render events during motion. Production preview produced about 172.2 render events/s, median interval 5.9 ms, p95 6.7 ms, p99 7.5 ms, maximum 8.7 ms and no interval above 33.4 ms in its approximately 9.3-second measured window. The earlier flat-model development baseline was 171.5/s with p95 7 ms. The final cold development campus flight produced 168.3/s, p95 7.6 ms, maximum 21 ms and no interval above 33.4 ms. These observations meet the 16.67 ms/60 fps frame budget on this adapter; they measure rendered-event cadence, not physical display refresh, and do not establish performance on other hardware, Safari or mobile GPUs.
