# Devlog — 2026-09-06, Phase 1

Data and provenance for the Shibuya deliverable. One session, one commit. The theme running through it: a research pass handed over a set of claims, four of which contradicted the plan, and the useful work was deciding which ones to believe on sight and which ones to re-measure.

## The plan's central premise about texture was inverted, and it had already been reasoned from

**Timestamp:** 2026-09-06 20:30–20:34

**Action:** The plan's Approach section said "PLATEAU supplies accurate geometry and almost no texture", and items 15 and 16 were both derived from it. A research pass claimed the opposite. Rather than take that on trust, parsed all four AOI building GMLs directly — 364 MB of XML — counting buildings whose `lod0RoofEdge` centroid falls inside the box, whether each has `lod2Solid` or `lod2MultiSurface`, and whether any of its polygon ids appears as an `app:target` in the file's appearance section.

**Result:** 1,741 buildings in the AOI box. 1,678 have LOD2 geometry and 1,678 carry a texture — 96.4%. **Zero** LOD2 buildings without a texture and **zero** textured buildings without LOD2. `uro:appearanceSrcDescLod2` is `1` (aerial photography) for exactly those 1,678 and `99` (not produced) for the other 63. This reproduces the research's figure to the building, from an independent parse.

**Reasoning:** The correction does not rescue the plan's strategy, and that is the part worth writing down. The textures are aerial photogrammetry, so they have daylight and hard shadows baked into the albedo and no emissive channel anywhere. Shibuya's signage is painted into a daytime diffuse map and will not glow. So item 16 (emissive signage) stays the highest-value item and dusk stays the hero preset — but item 15 flips from "procedural facades are the default treatment" to "PLATEAU texture is the albedo base for 96.4%, procedural is for the untextured 3.6% and hero close-ups", and a cost the plan never budgeted appears: de-lighting. That became item 35, appended rather than inserted, because the plan states its numbering is stable.

**Validation:** The count is reproducible from `data/plateau/udx/bldg/*.gml` after `npm run data:fetch`. It is not gated — a test that re-parses 364 MB is the wrong shape for `npm test`, and Phase 2 will have the parse in the pipeline anyway.

**Notes:** Two numbers where my measurement and the research's differ, both recorded in `design.md`.

The research said `bldg:measuredHeight` is "present on ~97%". It is present on **100%**, and **62 buildings (3.6%) carry the sentinel −9999**. Same fact, but the sentinel is the part that bites: a reader that does not filter it puts a building 9,999 m underground. `storeysAboveGround` has the same shape, with the sentinel 9999 on 298 buildings — including one real 172.4 m tower.

The research said a typical wall texture is "a 512×512 atlas", median 10 KiB, p90 37 KiB. Measured over all 2,634 JPEGs in mesh 53393596: **median 4,074 B, p90 25,952 B**, and the commonest sizes are 128×128, 256×256 and 64×64. The populations differ — the research measured the AOI box across four cells, which skews commercial and mid-rise — but the typical texture is smaller and lower-resolution than the report implies. That makes item 35 and item 15's hero-facade fallback more important, not less.

## The converter has no Windows CLI, and the alternative turned out to be better rather than merely adequate

**Timestamp:** 2026-09-06 20:27–20:33

**Action:** Plan item 10 assumed the PLATEAU GIS Converter is a tool you run. On Windows the release is a GUI installer; the CLI ships only for Linux and macOS, and upstream CI runs only on Ubuntu. The instruction was to default to MLIT's pre-converted 3D Tiles and take the Rust build only if a sample showed they lacked something. So: fetched the tileset, walked it, pulled two `.b3dm` tiles covering the crossing, and parsed them byte by byte against the CityGML.

**Result:** The tiles won on both criteria that matter most, not just tied.

Per-building attributes: a **63-key batch table**, plain JSON plus a small binary block, carrying `bldg:measuredHeight`, `bldg:storeysAboveGround`, `bldg:usage`, `gml_id`, `uro:lodType` and per-feature bounding boxes. Cross-checked against the CityGML on two named buildings — 渋谷ヒカリエ reads 173.6 m and 34 storeys in both, 渋谷ストリーム 171.3 m and 35 storeys in both. The converter's equivalent path, `EXT_structural_metadata`, has an open unfixed bug that makes Cesium throw.

Addressability: `BATCH_LENGTH` plus a `_BATCHID` vertex attribute, and a five-level `REPLACE` hierarchy of 730 content tiles of which **67 intersect the AOI**. That is item 12's culling unit already built. The converter emits **one GLB per feature *type*** — every building in one mesh — which is precisely the granularity item 12 must not have.

Texture: one 2048×2048 lossy WebP atlas per tile. Against the source, mesh 53393596's appearance directory is 184.4 Mpx across 2,634 JPEGs, and the 75 pre-converted tiles covering that cell carry 314.6 Mpx. So the route is not resolution-limited; the loss is one lossy re-encode.

**Reasoning:** The route's one real cost is that `CESIUM_RTC` is in `extensionsRequired` and three.js does not implement it — the loader warns and carries on, so a tile would sit at the origin. That had to be shown solvable before deciding, not after. `data503.b3dm`'s ECEF centre `[-3956953.773, 3355546.821, 3697608.127]` converts to 35.6595201 N, 139.7016341 E, which is 2.1 m north and 102.7 m east of the world origin — the right place. And a better route emerged from the same parse: the per-feature `_zmin`/`_zmax` in the batch table are already **orthometric** (14.86–15.70 m at the crossing), where the ECEF height is ellipsoidal (78.018 m). A pipeline can georeference from the batch table and never touch the geoid.

**Validation:** Not rendered. The Draco decode has been observed to be declared and never exercised, and that is written into `design.md` as the first thing Phase 2 should do. Two other things route B costs, also recorded: the LOD2 tileset is LOD2 only, so the 63 LOD1 buildings live in a separate tileset that must be merged on `gml_id`; and `bldg:measuredHeight` appears as a JSON array in one tile and a binary property in another, depending on nullability.

## Two gates that catch defects no frame review could

**Timestamp:** 2026-09-06 20:37–20:40

**Action:** Wrote `test/aoi.test.ts` and `test/elevation.test.ts`, then made each go red by feeding it the defect it exists for.

**Result:** The coordinate gate, with the two returns of `geographicToPlaneRectangular` transposed — the EPSG:6677 northing-first trap written out literally — failed 9 of 15, including `is not mirrored: Shibuya Scramble Square lands south-east of the crossing → expected -25873.97 to be greater than 0`.

The elevation gate went red twice, once per half. Adding the 36.8772 m geoid undulation to the TIN sample gave `expected 52.07583360361154 to be close to 15.2` and `the two surveys ... expected 36.89583360361154 to be less than 0.5`. Decoding the GSI tile as Mapbox Terrain-RGB gave `expected -9848.2 to be close to 15.18`.

**Reasoning:** Both defects are invisible in a rendered frame. A transposed Shibuya renders perfectly, at the right scale, with the right buildings, mirrored — and nobody in this fleet knows the skyline well enough to notice it backwards. Terrain uniformly 36.877 m too high looks exactly like terrain, with the buildings still sitting on it correctly. There are two independent chances to make the first mistake, because CityGML's `posList` is latitude-first and EPSG:6677 is northing-first.

**Validation:** One bound is worth naming because it is luck and not design. The "measures 997.1 by 997.3 metres" assertion also caught the transposition, reporting 997.297 against an expected 997.1. It could only do that because the AOI box happens to be 0.17 m wider than it is tall. A square AOI would have left that assertion blind to a transposition while still passing.

**Notes:** The projection itself was written from the Krüger series rather than borrowed, and it reproduces the research's four measured points — the crossing, both AOI corners and Shibuya Scramble Square — to the millimetre. Two independent implementations agreeing to 1 mm is better evidence than either alone.

## The attribution surface changed a number an existing gate depends on

**Timestamp:** 2026-09-06 20:52–21:00

**Action:** Item 8's attribution overlay replaces the placeholder `<div>` in `index.html`. The Phase 0 gate proof for "a frame that did not render" records that an empty scene measures a luminance spread of **2.84 against a floor of 6**, and says explicitly that it measures 2.84 rather than 0 *because the attribution overlay is drawn over it*. So the new overlay changes that bound. Re-ran the same mutation — the five `scene.add` calls wrapped so nothing is added — and measured.

**Result:** **2.20.** The failure read `street-az000.png is uniformly flat (luminance spread 2.20)`, exit 1, with real frames in the same build running 18.0 to 36.9. The gap either side of the floor widened rather than narrowed.

**Reasoning:** Worth doing rather than assuming, because the direction was not obvious in advance: the new surface has more text in it than the placeholder did. It came out lower because the credit line is dimmer and tighter than the placeholder's, and the details panel is closed by default. Had it come out at 5.5 the gate would still have passed every run while having quietly stopped separating "rendered" from "did not render", and nothing would have said so.

**Validation:** Recorded in the existing gate-proof entry as a re-measurement rather than a new entry, since it is the same gate with a changed bound. `src/ui/attribution.ts` carries the constraint in its own header so the next person to grow the overlay is told to re-measure.

**Notes:** Two things the frames themselves showed that the numbers did not. The credit was readable over the scene's dark road surfaces and faint over its pale rooftops, so it gained a text shadow — a shadow rather than a brighter colour, because a shadow raises contrast by adding dark pixels and does not raise a blank frame's luminance. And driving the real page in a browser showed the details panel opening **downward from a bottom-anchored credit line**, straight off the screen; it now opens upward. Neither was visible in the twelve gate frames, because the panel is closed in all of them.

## `data/` in .gitignore also ignored `tools/data/`, and git reported it as nothing at all

**Timestamp:** 2026-09-06 21:00

**Action:** Ran `git status --porcelain` before staging, and read the list rather than trusting it.

**Result:** `?? tools/geo/` was there. `?? tools/data/` was not — the directory holding `fetch.ts`, `manifest.ts` and `overpass-query.ts`, which is the whole of item 5's and item 6's deliverable. A bare `data/` pattern matches a directory of that name **at any depth**, so the rule written to exclude 620 MiB of downloads was also excluding the script that downloads them. Anchored to `/data/` and it appeared.

**Reasoning:** The failure mode is the dangerous shape: an ignored file is not reported as skipped, it is reported as absent. Staging by explicit pathspec would not have saved it either — `git add tools/data/fetch.ts` on an ignored path fails with a message, but in a longer `git add` list with other paths that message is one line among twenty warnings about CRLF. The commit would have landed referencing a script that was not in it, and `npm run data:fetch` would have failed on a fresh clone with `Cannot find module`.

**Validation:** `git check-ignore -v` on both a `data/` path and `tools/data/fetch.ts` — the first names the rule, the second exits 1. The staged tree was listed in full before committing and every intended file was in it.

## A fixture whose byte count changes on checkout is not pinned

**Timestamp:** 2026-09-06 21:01

**Action:** After committing, deleted both fixtures and restored them with `git checkout --`, which reproduces what a fresh clone gets, then re-ran the elevation gate.

**Result:** The gate passed, but `plateau-dem-crossing.gml` came back at **69,950 bytes** where 68,517 went in. `core.autocrlf` is true here, so Git had normalised the source file's CRLF into the blob and expanded it again on checkout. The PNG was correctly detected as binary and came back byte-identical.

**Reasoning:** The parser is whitespace-tolerant, so nothing broke. But the fixture's own README documented 68,517 bytes, and a pinned input whose length depends on the machine that checked it out is not pinned — it is just a file that happens to work. The next person to compare a hash would find a mismatch and have no way to tell a corrupted fixture from a line-ending conversion.

**Validation:** `test/fixtures/.gitattributes` now carries `* -text`, matching what `docs/work/` already does, and the GML was normalised to LF once so the pin is platform-neutral. The README records both fixtures' SHA-256. All five gates re-run green afterwards and all twelve frames looked at again.

## Things a later session could trip over

**The archive has two builds under one name.** The citable geospatial.jp resource is 649,322,807 bytes; the `13113-latest` alias 302s to a copy that is 649,325,272 bytes. Neither pass diffed them. `tools/data/manifest.ts` pins the first by hash. That alias also answers 404 to HEAD and 200 or 302 only to GET.

**`overpass-api.de` answers HTTP 406 to a User-Agent containing a parenthesised comment**, which is exactly the form OSM convention asks contact details be given in, and 406 is documented as meaning "rate limited". Two misleading signals in one response.

**`out body; >; out skel qt;` emits some elements twice**, tagged then bare. Anything indexing by id must keep the first occurrence; keeping the last silently discards every tag in the file.

**Node's type stripping does not rewrite a `.js` specifier to the `.ts` file beside it.** `tools/data/` is run by bare Node and therefore imports with real `.ts` extensions, which needed `allowImportingTsExtensions` in `tsconfig.json`. Everything Vite or Vitest resolves keeps the repo's `.js` convention. The failure if you forget is `ERR_MODULE_NOT_FOUND` naming a `.js` file that was never going to exist.

**`src/world/frame.ts` claimed EPSG:6677 northings around Shibuya are about 8,000,000 m.** They are about −37,768 m — CS IX's origin is at 36 N, only 38 km away. That is the geocentric figure, not the plane rectangular one. The comment is corrected; the reason for a local origin still stands, it is just smaller than the comment claimed.
