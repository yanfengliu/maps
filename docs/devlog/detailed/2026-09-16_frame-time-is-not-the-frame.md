# 2026-09-16 — the frame is not the problem, the simulation is

Phase 9 asked for 60 fps at 1920x1080 with 3,000 animated pedestrians and 200 vehicles. The measurement says the renderer has more than an order of magnitude of headroom and the CPU simulation is what misses, and the shape of the failure is not a slow frame but a catch-up loop.

**Correction, same session.** The lane re-ran to its own protocol and superseded its first report, so the numbers below are the final ones; the first message's figures are noted where they differ, because both windows are in the report and the range is the run-to-run spread.

## What was measured

At 1920x1080 with 3,000 pedestrians drawn, the camera driven by synthesised pointer and wheel input: frame cost **p50 0.900 ms, p95 3.600 ms** over the 1,312 frames that contain the city and the crowd, with a mean of 20,299 instance slots a frame; pooled over all 3,883 frames, p50 0.800 / p95 3.500 / p99 4.600 / max 6.700 ms. That is 5.4% of the 16.667 ms interval at the median and 21.6% at p95, and the first window read 1.100 / 2.100 ms. **The target holds with about 13.1 ms of slack.** Hiding the entire agent group costs 0.1 ms *less*; hiding the whole city costs 0.2 ms less; the cost tracks the post chain's full-screen passes over 470 MB of targets.

The population curve is flat: 1,000 pedestrians 0.800 ms, 2,000 0.800, 3,000 0.900, 4,000 0.900, while mean instance slots go from 8,259 to 26,157. Quadrupling the crowd costs 0.1 ms, which is the fence's own floor. So **3,000 is not the largest population that fits and at 1080p on this GPU the question has no largest population** — the population is not the term that consumes the interval.

The term that is: the whole-app interval measured **54.4 to 98.0 ms**, about 10 to 18 fps, with the simulation burning **33 to 69 ms a frame** and pinned at the render loop's five-step catch-up cap. The fixed step costs about 12.8 ms headless, so it sits near the interval; once it does, the loop tries to catch up, spends several steps inside one frame, and the frame time becomes a multiple of the step. This is a feedback loop, and a marginal improvement to the step does not escape it — the step has to come down far enough that catch-up cannot engage. Neither window is vsync-clamped; both were taken under heavy process load and are contended measurements rather than frame rates.

## The instrument had to be rebuilt, and five ways it lied

**`EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` is advertised on this machine and does not work.** `gl.createQuery()` returns `INVALID_ENUM` (1280) on all three ANGLE backends in a bare context. In the app's own context the query is accepted and resolves thousands of times, and **3,906 of the 4,382 queries that arm resolved answered 0 ms** while the interval was 35 ms. **Corrected 2026-09-16:** this line read "every one of 3,906 results answered 0 ms", which reads the zero count as the resolved count. The lane's own report publishes that arm as `queriesResolved: 4382, queryError: 0, zeroResults: 3906` (`artifacts/frame-time/wt/artifacts/frame-time/REPORT.md`, SHA-256 `24FF2E61EE0B1D5408CD68085638C5BF5DC59754AD383A084EDB639E2CDF4CA2`), and the arm's own log records `queriesResolved: 4382` beside `losses: {"resolved after its record was handed over": 1286}` (`scratch-query.log` in the same directory, SHA-256 `53C7AB92F0B67885F94FC6772E6FA6289583E12ABDC9B9EE427BDBF1B7A670FA`), so 3,906 of 4,382 resolved results answered 0 ms and 1,286 were lost before their record was handed over. The 3,906-of-3,906 shape belongs to the other two arms, `run-final.log` (2,262 of 2,262) and `run-acceptance.log` (2,711 of 2,711). Any lane that checks `getExtension() !== null`, or that a query resolved, reports 0 ms per frame as a measurement. The perf-ceiling report's statement that the extension "is advertised on both renderers" is true and is not evidence that it works — the same species as the stale gate header this session already had to correct. Every number above comes from a `gl.finish()` plus 1x1 `readPixels` fence, verified out of process against a known-cost draw (heavy 4.4-4.6 ms against cheap 0.2-0.5 ms).

**A population curve that measured an almost empty scene.** The first attempt reported cost *falling* with population, from 1.100 ms at 1,000 down to 0.100 ms at 6,000, because draws p50 was 1 and instances 0. All three existing population gates passed while this was happening, because **the renderer draws what has spawned rather than what was requested**. The gate now waits on `rendered.pedestrians`, the report lists five further instrument defects that each reported a fast frame, and a `?__probe=` sensitivity design was dropped because broken shaders draw nothing.

## What this changes about the plan

The performance criterion is not a renderer problem and should not be worked as one. Moving the per-frame instance composition to the GPU, which the perf report proposed as the place the "anything on the GPU" direction pays, is **not worth doing for frame rate**: it costs 0 ms at the median and 14.1 ms at p95 of main-thread time, and removing the agents does not move the frame. What the criterion now needs is the fixed step far below the interval.

Two population facts sit beside this and are not performance questions. The app requests 200 vehicles and draws **59 to 60**, because the bus class is refused at every scale the fleet randomises — no AOI entry portal's junction sections are long enough for its footprint radius — so a third of the fleet's slots are dead demand and every figure above is at 60 vehicles. **Corrected 2026-09-16:** this line read "2,859 bus spawns are refused", and 2,859 is in no record. The record keeps these per scale, and the lane's own acceptance windows report the modal scale 1.002 at **2,806** at the sync window's close and **2,208** in the async window, with the whole class at **5,862** across the 41 scales at the sync close against 1,795 at its open (`artifacts/frame-time/wt/artifacts/frame-time/raw-acceptance-3000-200-sync.json`, SHA-256 `0E7095D31408484CA16CE0E0F76EC201488531D21236A5D427BA9D7BBFF6B7DE`; `raw-acceptance-3000-200.json` beside it). Over the judged 360 s the same class at scale 1.002 reaches **17,888** (`artifacts/g23-prep/acc-centre-21600.json:55-59`, SHA-256 `7BB27A3CB1DB737A47C5C48BBD40F5FC6647FD7438F1DC096B45EEDDB5C2520C`). And a `hasReceivingSpace` check that decides capacity is vacuous for vehicles, because the active vehicles hold distinct route objects and its identity test matches nobody.
