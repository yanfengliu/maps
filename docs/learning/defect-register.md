## 2026-09-17 — the flythrough's approach leg swung the long way round, so the crowd leg opened on a hill face

Observed symptom (the flythrough run of 2026-09-16, `artifacts/flythrough2/adjudication-report.md`): `frames/crowd/crowd-000.png was captured with the camera -0.6 m above the highest terrain under it (-383, -479)`. The clearance check that reported it is correct and is untouched.

Investigation: the crowd leg's frames were not the defect. The camera reached the crowd's corner 3.5–4.4 m lower and about 17 m off the scored pose, and one capped stand correction of about 1.1 m plus the 0.3 m hold clearance cannot cover that. The pose is set by the approach leg's last step, and that step's bearing was 0.9381 rad short: the approach interpolated its swing from `OPENING_AZIMUTH` 0.7854 to `CROWD_AZIMUTH` 2.2253 through `+ 2*PI`, which is 7.7231 rad over six steps, 1.2872 rad a step, while the driver buys a bearing with a left-button drag clamped at `maxRotatePx` — 40 px, or 0.3491 rad at this lane's 1280x720 canvas. The driver delivered 0.3491 a step and its `shortestAngle` arithmetic flipped sign twice, so the approach ended at azimuth 1.2872 against the 2.2253 the crowd leg's first frame needed. The plan's comment claimed a "172 degree swing" and that the long way made the driver's shortest-angle arithmetic agree; the swing is 82.5 degrees and the unrolled form is what broke it.

Root cause: a commanded angle written outside the range the control can deliver in one step, with no check that the sequence converges. Nothing in the run reported a shortfall: `turnTo` returns `remainingRad` and the leg drives on, so the failure surfaced two legs later as a terrain clearance, at the wrong end of the route.

Fix: the swing is a monotone interpolation towards `CROWD_AZIMUTH` — 1.4399 rad, 82.5 degrees, 0.2400 rad a step — and the stale arithmetic in the plan and in `turnStepRad`'s own doc is rewritten to the real numbers.

Checked from now on: `test/flythrough-plan.test.ts` flies the plan's own `LEGS` through `rotateStepRadians` and `shortestAngle` from `tools/flythrough/driver.ts` at the capture viewport and requires the approach to end within 0.05 rad of `CROWD_AZIMUTH` and the ascent within 0.05 rad of `OPENING_AZIMUTH`. Restoring the long way round makes it exit 1 with `the approach leg delivers the camera to azimuth 1.2872 rad and the crowd leg's first frame needs 2.2253 ... expected 0.9381162336791848 to be less than 0.05`, which is the delivered bearing the failed run had. The ascent's same unwrapped form was measured and converges at step 10 with one step spare, so it is unchanged. Record: `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`, "The flythrough's approach swing reaches the crowd's bearing through the drag the driver delivers". Bound: the pure rotate arithmetic and the plan's own azimuth targets at `maxRotatePx` 40 and a 720 px canvas — not the browser's input path, not the damping tail, and not the pose the controls settle at, which only a run of the lane can show.

## 2026-09-16 — the dusk post chain's temporal accumulation never engaged, because the camera it waits for could never stop

Observed symptom (gate-timing lane, `artifacts/gate-timing/REPORT.md`): every one of the eight hero frames of the 44-frame capture recorded `taaAccumulating: false, taaSamples: 0`, reproduced on both renderers. The plan's acceptance criterion asks the dusk preset to render with TAA and to hold still; the mechanism was present in the code and absent from every pixel, and nothing failed.

Investigation: the post chain was not at fault. `src/app.ts` decides the camera holds still by comparing the sixteen elements of `camera.matrixWorld` against 1e-7, and hands that answer to the accumulator. An `OrbitControls` camera on damping is a first-order lag: each `update()` applies `dampingFactor` of the residual and multiplies what is left by `1 - dampingFactor`, so the glide decays by 0.92 a frame and reaches zero only when the arithmetic runs out of significand. Measured at the hero crossing pose on the RTX 4090: the shutter reads the accumulator at frame 378 with the camera still gliding at 8.96e-5 m a frame, which is 0.0014 of a pixel — the picture was already still and the predicate still said no — and the glide crossed the 1e-7 bar 200 frames after the last pointer input, 82 frames after the shutter.

Root cause: a stillness test strict enough that a damped camera can never satisfy it, read by the one consumer that needs a genuinely stationary camera. The defect was in the producer of the state, not in the post chain that consumes it, and no gate could see it because the visual gate never asserted the accumulator and the criterion was only ever checked by reading a report.

Fix: `src/render/controls-rest.ts` ends the glide when everything the residual still holds could not move the picture by one pixel at the pivot distance, stepping the control once with damping off so both residuals are zeroed outright, installed from `src/render/camera.ts`. The 1e-7 bar is untouched. Raising that bar was rejected with a reason: it makes the predicate agree with the damping instead of making the damping stop, and it would still be a race against the harness's shutter.

Checked from now on: `test/camera-rest.test.ts` drives real `OrbitControls` through its real pointer handlers and requires the app's own 1e-7 witness to turn true within 120 frames and to stay true for 200 more. Made red by removing the `installControlRest` call from `src/render/camera.ts` and rebuilding: `the camera was still moving after 120 frames, so the app's own stillness witness — the one the post chain's temporal accumulation reads — never turns true and no captured frame can carry a single accumulated sample.: expected -1 to be greater than 0`. The rendered check is `tools/post-chain/motion.spec.ts`: fifteen real pointer drags and then the stop, every frame captured at native 1280x720, asserting from the PNG bytes that a held camera draws two byte-identical frames 500 ms apart and that across 711 recorded frames zero accumulated samples came from more than one camera pose — the anti-smear property measured rather than read off the code. Three of those frames were opened at native size and are digest-bound in `artifacts/post-chain/REPORT.md`.

Numbers: at the shutter `false / 0` becomes `true / 32`, and the first accumulating frame moves from 460 to 185. Bound: the fix is measured on the hardware renderer only, and the 44-frame verdict sweep runs on SwiftShader, so whether the accumulator engages there is not established by this measurement; in a populated run `src/app.ts` keeps the accumulator off whenever agents are moving, so TAA does not reach a populated frame by construction, which is code rather than a measurement; and there is no no-TAA pixel A/B, because `options.post` has no URL switch. No independent review of this change.

## 2026-09-16 — a queue's vehicles interpenetrated, and nothing measured the space between them

Observed symptom (independent review, round 29, first blocking finding): vehicles in a queue occupied the same road space — **120 oriented-box overlapping pairs among 71 active bodies** at 200 vehicles and 3,600 ticks, deepest longitudinal overlap **4.147 m at a 0.500 m origin gap**, two taxis — while every counter the population published read normally. Three lanes had reported the vehicle population as working because none of them read space: `tools/agents/population-run.ts` at `5e8c266^` publishes the lifecycle counts, the admission counters, the longest waits and the same-tick cost, and a grep of it for overlap, spacing, gap or collision terms returns nothing, while `test/vehicle-trajectory.test.ts`'s `sweepsOverlap` cases are about a planned path's swept footprint rather than two bodies in the running simulation.

Investigation: the fix in `5e8c266` made the measure part of the type. `Leader` carries a `kind`: `body` is another vehicle whose `gapM` is the free distance between the two collision envelopes, with the fleet's own `standstillClearanceM` as its standstill room; `halt` is a point on this body's own route and keeps the contract's `stopGapM`, because the planner has already placed that point a whole footprint radius short and the halt request window is written against the origin. Naming the kind is what stops a caller handing one measure to the other, since each constructor supplies its own constant, and the gate's boxes are the delivered collision envelopes (kei 1.920 x 3.573 m, taxi 2.140 x 4.573 m, bus 3.280 x 10.565 m) rather than a distance between origins. Two further mechanisms the review's diagnosis did not reach were found by fixing the first and re-measuring: `spawnClearance` prefiltered on arc length, so a lane section curving back on itself let a spawn land 2.28 m from a body it believed was 7.15 m away, and it tested only bodies holding an active byte, leaving a body staged at a portal invisible; and `HALT_CAPTURE_M` was a margin rather than part of the clearance, so a taxi's 1.457 m hop onto its own first halt closed a guard-approved 5.6 m of clearance to 4.55 m between two 4.573 m taxis. Measured on the fix at 200 vehicles and 3,600 ticks: 0 overlapping pairs and 0 penetration, following clearance median +3.419 m and min +1.945 m against -3.426 m and -4.147 m before. The cost is stated rather than buried: vehicle crossings fell 311 to 280 and completions 203 to 172, about 10 to 13 percent, because bodies that had been overlapping were sharing road space and the queue is now physically real.

Root cause: a unit error in the contract's own vocabulary. The car-following law read the network contract's 0.5 m `stopGapM` — the gap a body keeps to a stop line — as if it separated two bodies, and measured its own gap origin to origin, so a queue's standstill equilibrium was half a metre between centres and every class in the fleet overlapped itself by roughly its own length. No test could see it, because the law was implemented as written and nothing measured the space the bodies occupied in a running population.

Checked from now on: `test/vehicle-spacing.test.ts`, landed with the fix in `5e8c266`, drives the delivered network and fleet and asserts that no two vehicles travelling the same direction overlap as oriented collision boxes, measured by `tools/agents/spacing-metrics.ts`; the head-on folded-lane class is admitted only where the two lane centrelines come within the two bodies' own half lengths, and any other head-on overlap fails. On `f4e96f0` the mutation — the origin-to-origin separation restored in `buildVehicleFrame`'s clearance, `delta` and `ahead` in place of the two `- halfLengths` forms — fails the case at tick 475 with `kei#15 into taxi#17, 0.120 m of oriented-box overlap at a -4.009 m origin gap, headings 0.0 deg apart`, exit status 1 in 2.96 s, and the same mutation through `node tools/agents/vehicle-spacing.ts --vehicles 200 --pedestrians 0 --ticks 3600` exits 1 with 31 same-direction pairs and 2.224 m of deepest penetration (taxi#27 into taxi#98) against 0 and 0 on the fixed revision. Record: `docs/learning/gate-proofs.md`, "The vehicle spacing gate catches bodies that interpenetrate". Bound: one delivered network at seed `0x5b1b0a`, 120 vehicles and no pedestrians, 1,200 ticks (20 simulated seconds) sampled every 5 ticks — long enough for queues to form at the portals and the first gates, not a full signal cycle — and nothing about pedestrian spacing, lane changes at junctions, route completion or the 3,000-pedestrian acceptance load. The pedestrian half of the same review finding, **46,615 overlapping pairs at t = 60 s**, is not gated on this revision: `test/pedestrian-overlap.test.ts` is untracked and unlanded in `artifacts/spacing/wt`, and the fix for it was held back because it deadlocked the crowd.

**One figure is recorded three ways and they are not three defects.** The deepest longitudinal overlap reads 4.147 m at a 0.500 m origin gap in `5e8c266`'s message, 4.687 m at the same origin gap in the spacing lane's unlanded entry, and 4.657 m at a 0.045 m origin gap under the minimal mutation on this revision. They are the same class sampled at different ticks and revisions; the 0.500 m gap is the contract's `stopGapM` showing through, which is the root cause itself.

## 2026-09-15 - navigating away from the refined city stalls about 28-30 s on a software rasteriser

Observed symptom: on a machine drawing with SwiftShader, leaving the refined city for another URL freezes the page for roughly half a minute before the replacement document starts. The 15 s navigation bound in `tools/visual/lifecycle.spec.ts` fails, and the user-visible effect is the same stall.

Investigation: an init script timed the application's own `pagehide` listener on the absolute page clock while a stage localisation and three probe arms measured where the interval sits. The application's cleanup (`picker.dispose()` plus `app.dispose()`) spans 2.4 ms with no context loss, 4.3 ms of app work when `WEBGL_lose_context.loseContext()` is forced inside `pagehide` (that handler then spans 30,453 ms), and 2.1 ms on the hardware arm. On the hardware renderer the same unchanged check navigates in 32-110 ms.

Root cause: Chromium's destruction of the outgoing page's WebGL resources on the renderer main thread under SwiftShader. The mechanism - deleting resources versus draining in-flight work - is not separated by this evidence, and the cost is not shown to be independent of the scene's resource count (the 2026-09-13 trace of the same failure recorded 65 loaded tiles, `cachedBytes` 630,155,033, `gpuBytes` 317,190,172). The application's own cleanup is excluded as the cause; it is milliseconds in every arm.

How it is checked from now on: the lifecycle check runs on the hardware renderer the deliverable targets (`playwright.lifecycle.config.ts`, part of `npm run visual`), records the unmasked renderer string each run, and fails by name if that string names a software rasteriser. The software baseline stays reproducible as disclosed counterevidence through the ignored diagnostic config and the preserved trace, and this bound must be re-measured when the scene changes materially.
