# Defect register

## 2026-09-17 — the detail tile was in the bundle and invisible in the pixels, and the black plaza near field was asphalt rather than light

Observed symptom, from the eight inspection reports under `artifacts/inspection-gpu2/` bound to certificate runId `fb07b11ea3b8296f` (the re-capture after the tile landed in `c0d0f7c`): every ground and road crop in the hero approach frames was unchanged from the pre-tile capture beyond dither, structure energy within 1.2% and whole-frame mean difference 0.004–0.28/255; the cartographic plaza's aimed cell measured **38% less** local structure than before the tile; and the satellite plaza's `az000` near field was **black** — 66.6% of the `near-pavement` crop below luminance 12, 98.3% of 8×8 blocks dead flat — while the same frame's right-hand ground showed a full paving grid and a tactile strip. The reports recorded the leading hypothesis for the first as "the `onBeforeCompile` shader patch may be a silent no-op if its `String.replace` anchor does not match the shader chunk three.js actually compiles", and the second as "the light/falloff or the mip fade", because the same material looked legible on one side of one frame and flat on the other.

Investigation, by measurement.

**The patch is applied, and the hypothesis is false.** All four anchors — `void main() {` in both stages, `#include <project_vertex>`, `#include <roughnessmap_fragment>` — occur exactly once each in three r180's `ShaderLib.standard`, which is what `MeshStandardMaterial` compiles. The positive control is in the frames the reports themselves inspected: the basket-weave paving joints at 0.6 × 0.4 m come from this same patch's `sidewalk` branch, which has no texture and no other source, and they are legible in every capture before and after the tile landed. A patch that had gone silent would have taken those joints with it.

**The tile renders and its contrast is two orders of magnitude short of a display level.** Measuring the shipped bytes gives a mip-0 mean neighbouring-texel step of **0.36% of albedo on the road and 0.40% on the ground** — the inspection's own figures, reproduced independently here — and the renderer's curve (ACES filmic at the style's exposure, then sRGB) turns a relative albedo change of 1% into about 0.36 of a 0-255 level. So 0.36% is 0.13 of a level, and the frames were unchanged because there was nothing to see. Two independent mistakes produced that number. First, the octave amplitudes (1, 0.62, 0.38, 0.22, 0.12, 0.06) over lattices of 4 to 128 cells decay faster than the lattices refine, so the field was a smooth 8 m hill whose local step was 2.3% of its own range. Second, and not recorded in the reports: every texel sampled a lattice cell *midpoint* (`u = (x + 0.5) / SIZE`), so with 128 cells each texel centre landed on `fx = 0.5`, where smoothstep's slope is zero — the finest octave was written as the average of two neighbouring lattice values, halved and smoothed before it was sampled. A further fact makes the regression concrete: the tile commit **removed** the road's previous `grain * 0.025` sine term and replaced it with 0.36%, so the near-field asphalt lost about seven eighths of the structure it had. That is why the cartographic plaza's aimed cell measured 38% less structure after the tile than before it.

**The black near field is the road mesh, and the "position-dependent" premise was a region-labelling error.** The inspection tool names its bottom row `left-ground` and `near-pavement`, and both reports read those names as material claims. They are grid positions. At the plaza pose the camera stands on the avenue, so the near field and the left-hand bottom band are the PLATEAU `tran` road surface (`roads:plateau-tran`), and the paving grid that reads as healthy is the pavement mesh on the right (`roads:plateau-semantic-pavement`) — 0x32363c against 0x999991 in the satellite style, a tenfold albedo difference between two meshes drawn in one frame. Measured from the frames for this entry: satellite near-field road median 10.4 and 66.6% under 12 (reproducing the report exactly), satellite right-ground pavement median 51.3, cartographic near-field road median 52.4. Light cannot be the mechanism: `lighting.ts` stops the key light casting shadows below 8 degrees of solar elevation and this preset is at −3.9, so no shadow exists that could fall differently across one horizontal plane; and the road mesh's stored normals are 100% upward-facing, measured, so it is not a flipped-normal surface lit from below.

Root cause, in three parts. The tile's generator had no bound on the quantity that decides visibility — the neighbouring-texel step in albedo, which is the tile's local step times the shader's strength — so a change that satisfied "carries structure at more than one scale" shipped invisible and its own unit cases were green. Second, the sampling offset systematically averaged the finest octave away, so the spectrum that was chosen could not have reached the pixel even at a higher strength. Third, the inspection's own region names were read as materials, which turned a two-mesh albedo difference into a search for a light falloff that does not exist; the road's near-black albedo (linear 0.032, below the range asphalt occupies at all) is the whole of that defect.

Fix: `cd94f1a` (the tile's spectra, corner-aligned sampling, anisotropy 8, and `patchSurfaceShader` extracted as a pure function that refuses a source missing an anchor) and `a2282f8` (the satellite road's albedo 0x32363c → 0x474d55). Shipped, the road's tile puts a mean 6.35% of albedo between neighbouring texels at mip 0 (p95 14.8%) and 3.97% one mip down, where the far end of the hero crossing's 0.0144–0.0886 m/px sits; the ground's is 3.38%.

Checked from now on, with the bound of each check stated. `test/surface-material-shader.test.ts` restates the four anchor literals rather than importing them, holds the injected marker landing after the call site in both stages, holds every bound uniform being declared, and holds the refusal itself for a missing or duplicated anchor; its bound is that it drives the patch against three's source text and the material's own `onBeforeCompile` and never compiles a program, so it proves the source three is handed carries the detail term where the surface's colour is decided and not that the GPU links it. `test/surface-detail.test.ts` holds the *product* — the neighbouring-texel step in albedo, at mip 0 and at mip 1, per surface, against a floor derived from the display-level arithmetic and naming the old 0.36% in the failure message; its bound is that the floors come from the renderer's tone curve at the luminances the certified frames show, so they predict a level change rather than measuring one. `test/road-tone.test.ts` calibrates on the two certified near-field road measurements, holds the replaced albedo outside the asphalt band as a control inside the case, keeps the photographic style's road inside it, and keeps every style's road above its black end; its bound is that it gates the albedo and not the pixel.

**Result of the re-capture, runId `09e00eb5689892e0` (same adapter and driver, scene digest equal at `1ebda662…` over 117 files and 301,674,209 bytes, harness untouched, 44 frames certified).** The changed frames are `sweep/satellite/plaza-az000.png` `d19e6c32…` → `70179de0…`, `sweep/cartographic/plaza-az000.png` `a2ef4b86…` → `7565ee7c…`, `hero/hero-satellite-noon-crossing.png` `486cfd6e…` → `7d9e6f09…`, `hero/hero-cartographic-noon-crossing.png` `4fc42caa…` → `3e83527d…`, `hero/hero-cartographic-dusk-crossing.png` `1c121af0…` → `27183339…`.

*The road asphalt.* Measured on the near-field road band at the plaza pose — frame rect 340,600 600x100, which the camera's own geometry puts at 0.017–0.029 m/px, inside the acceptance bar's 0.0144–0.0886: the cartographic road's residual median goes **0.024 → 0.365**, its fraction of pixels indistinguishable from flat **99.9% → 63.5%**, and its mean neighbouring-pixel difference **0.038 → 0.531**, a 14x rise, with the band's mean luminance unchanged at 51.83 → 51.88 — the tile varies the surface around the palette's own mean, which is what it was built to do. The satellite band moves the same way but from a darker start: mean luminance 10.04 → 16.45, flat 100% → 96.5%, neighbouring difference 0.025 → 0.132. Looked at rather than measured: the cartographic plaza crop was a smooth blue-grey gradient and is now a surface with visible aggregate mottle across its whole width; that is the finding answered, by eye at native size. No crop shows a repeat lattice at either tile scale, which the energy measurement predicted (3.1% of the tile's RMS at the 2 m scale).

*Within the azimuth-45 hero crossing frames themselves the answer is weaker, and the reason is arithmetic rather than a second defect.* At that azimuth the camera stands on the pavement, so those frames' near field is the sidewalk (untouched) and their asphalt is the mid-ground band: measured on rect 10,350 290x90 it improves from residual median 0.477 → 0.904 and flat 51.6% → 30.1% in the cartographic noon frame, 0.325 → 0.588 and 59.3% → 44.9% at cartographic dusk, and 0.238 → 0.428 with 59.8% → 53.6% at satellite noon. That asphalt sits at roughly 0.07–0.3 m/px, so the 4 m tile is at mip 2–4 where the shipped spectrum gives 2.7% to 1.5% of albedo per texel — about one display level. By eye I can see the added mottling in the cartographic dusk pair and not confidently in the satellite ones. So requirement 1 is met for road asphalt at the bar's distances on the surface the plaza pose puts under the camera, and only partly met inside the azimuth-45 crossing frames, where the mip chain has already averaged the grain to under a level.

*The black near field: not met.* The satellite near-field road reads median luminance 10.4 → **16.6** and its under-12 population 66.6% → 0.3%, so the black *threshold* is cleared — but 16.6 is below the 20 floor this change's own gate names, and it sits at the **bottom of the predicted 15.6–24.6 range**. That is the two-point fit being right: the albedo-independent term (wet specular from the dusk sky plus bloom) really is most of that surface's radiance, so a 2x albedo lift buys +6 levels and not +20. By eye the crop is a dark surface rather than a black void, and it still reads as flat: I cannot call it textured asphalt. **The palette is therefore not this defect's lever, and the next change is the light on horizontal surfaces at dusk** — the pre-registered criterion, applied rather than restated. The albedo value is left in place for now because it is independently justified as physical (0x32363c is below the range asphalt occupies) and it removed the below-12 population, but it does not close the item and it should not be tuned further to chase the floor.

*No regression seen.* At the block and overhead framings the whole-frame differences are at or under 1.2 levels with nothing above 8, except the intended road tone lift (satellite block near-pavement +2.05 mean, 42.1% of pixels moved more than 2 and none more than 8). The cartographic near-white look survives: the noon crossing's asphalt band mean luminance is 169.80 → 169.79, the plaza road 51.83 → 51.88, and the paving, zebra bands, yellow diamond, signage and attribution line are all present and legible in the frames opened. The model's prediction was validated against the render (measured 16.6 inside the predicted 15.6–24.6), so the two-point fit tracks the renderer.

**Still unmeasured.** Temporal shimmer: every frame in evidence is a still, so the stronger fine content and the tile's roughness modulation at grain scale have not been watched in motion. And the ground's own 8 m tile at the block and overhead distances has been measured only as a whole-frame difference, not inspected crop by crop.

## 2026-09-17 — two lanes wrote the primary's served scene through a junction, and the scene digest turned out to be a build identity rather than a content identity

Observed symptom: `node tools/visual/verify-output.ts --end` reported the served scene moving under the certified frames — "84 files digested `ddd21ee1b4fe` when the run began and 117 files digest `e7c82765fd30` now" — after nothing had been intended to touch `data/`. Three separate causes were found, and one of them is a design fault that outlives the incident.

Investigation, in the order the evidence established it. (1) A Phase B lane's worktree had `data` **junctioned** to the primary checkout, as this repo's lane convention says, and it then ran `npm run data:scene` and `npm run data:markings` inside that worktree — so the writes landed in the primary's served scene. Its second `data:scene` failed after `cleanSceneGeometry` had already deleted the scene, leaving it briefly incomplete. (2) `npm run data:markings` re-derives `data/network/network.json` from the live OSM extract, so it broke that file's Review 10 pin (`314fac84…` → `e461ddc1…`) — the behaviour the plan already records as correct-but-expected for today's extract, reproduced here by a command whose name does not suggest it. (3) Two consecutive `data:scene` runs were diffed file by file: 114 of 117 files were byte-identical, and the three that moved were `manifest.json` (which carries `"builtAt"`), `control-hardware.json` and `agents/vehicles.json` — the latter two only because the lane's modified code had written them and the committed pipeline then overwrote them with its own deterministic output, which two committed runs reproduce exactly.

Root cause, in two parts. The immediate one is the junction: the convention that makes `data/` and `node_modules/` cheap to share also makes every data-writing command write into the primary, and the lane followed the convention it was given. **The lead's own assignment caused this** — it told the lane to junction `data` and then to re-run `npm run data:scene`, which cannot be done safely together. The structural one is the digest: `sceneTreeDigest` folds each file's bytes, and `data/scene/manifest.json` contains a build timestamp, so **any rebuild moves the scene digest even when every geometry byte is identical**. That makes the digest a build identity, not a content identity: a reviewer cannot tell a real content change from a rebuild, and the certificate's scene binding can never be re-derived after a rebuild — which is exactly the check that exists to catch a scene changing under a capture.

Recovery, completed and verified: the pinned network was restored from one of twelve surviving copies (the newest in `artifacts/graphics-facade-increment/worktree/`; the backup this session took also held it), and `test/paint-support.test.ts` then passed 6 of 6 with the authored markings, which is what identified the network — not the markings — as the failing pin. `npm test` is green at **61 files / 453 tests**. The scene's content now matches the committed pipeline's deterministic output for the pinned inputs: `terrain.mesh` `fef57d09…` is the pre-incident file, `roads.mesh` `3fe126cd…` and `pavements.mesh` `5d2a9150…` are their reviewed pins, `markings.mesh` `efdba9cb…` (388,992 bytes) is the authored step's output, and the buildings decode from the pinned archive with the build's own cross-check reporting 0 disagreements. What cannot be restored is the certificate's digest, because the pre-incident `builtAt` is gone; the digest now reads `20ac847450f9…` and the Phase B re-capture will re-issue the certificate against it. The pre-incident bytes of `buildings/`, `control-hardware.json` and `agents/vehicles.json` were never recorded, so content-identity with the pre-incident tree is inferred from determinism and the pin checks rather than byte-diffed, and that limit is stated rather than glossed. A backup of the whole tree as it stood after the incident is at `artifacts/scene-backup-20260917/`.

Checked from now on: two gates are owed and named here. (a) A lane that runs any data-writing command must not have `data` junctioned — it copies the tree it needs, or writes to an explicit output directory, and the local rules say so. (b) `sceneTreeDigest` must not fold a build timestamp: either the manifest's `builtAt` leaves the served payload (a build record belongs elsewhere) or the digest normalises it out, so that a rebuild and a content change can be told apart and the certificate's scene binding survives a rebuild. Until (b) lands, every scene rebuild strands the certificate's scene binding, and the runbook must say so.

Root cause: a stillness test strict enough that a damped camera can never satisfy it, read by the one consumer that needs a genuinely stationary camera. The defect was in the producer of the state, not in the post chain that consumes it, and no gate could see it because the visual gate never asserted the accumulator and the criterion was only ever checked by reading a report.

The 1e-7 bar is untouched. Raising that bar was rejected with a reason: it makes the predicate agree with the damping instead of making the damping stop, and it would still be a race against the harness's shutter.

Checked from now on: `test/camera-rest.test.ts` drives real `OrbitControls` through its real pointer handlers and requires the app's own 1e-7 witness to turn true within 120 frames and to stay true for 200 more. The replacement document's own document-start script does not run until +27.87 s, and the stage localisation showed the wait failing at 15,016 ms even with `waitUntil: "commit"`. On the hardware renderer the same unchanged check navigates in 32-110 ms.

Root cause: Chromium's destruction of the outgoing page's WebGL resources on the renderer main thread under SwiftShader. The mechanism - deleting resources versus draining in-flight work - is not separated by this evidence, and the cost is not shown to be independent of the scene's resource count (the 2026-09-13 trace of the same failure recorded 65 loaded tiles, `cachedBytes` 630,155,033, `gpuBytes` 317,190,172). The application's own cleanup is excluded as the cause; it is milliseconds in every arm.

How it is checked from now on: the lifecycle check runs on the hardware renderer the deliverable targets (`playwright.lifecycle.config.ts`, part of `npm run visual`), records the unmasked renderer string each run, and fails by name if that string names a software rasteriser. A machine with no usable GPU reports the check unavailable and red - never a skip and never a pass. The software baseline stays reproducible as disclosed counterevidence through the ignored diagnostic config and the preserved trace, and this bound must be re-measured when the scene changes materially.
