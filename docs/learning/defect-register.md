## 2026-10-20 — Benches do not match campus seating

Symptom: the user reports that benches are inaccurate. Investigation confirms all benches used the same wood model even when material tags specified concrete; unknown backrest/material and road-derived facing were treated as detailed geometry. The node-only source extraction also omitted mapped bench ways and curved seating. Correction and verification are tracked in [the bench/sculpture record](../work/0_bay-area-stanford/design.md#bench-accuracy-and-campus-sculpture): preserve source facts, use photo-supported forms, distinguish unknowns, check actual line paths and facing semantics, and inspect native close views.

Root cause: the first environment renderer drew every bench point as one invented design. It read OSM `direction` as the seat's long axis rather than the seated facing, ignored `material`, added a back unless `backrest=no`, and the generator read only nodes. Review 9 of the 2026-10-29 integration found the class again in the new way renderer: 23 mapped ways with no material became solid concrete-toned seats, and a bent way's back switched sides between segments. The generator's facing estimate was the third form. It measured a way from its middle node, an end node for a two-node way. A bench touching a footway read the footway as its facing; `way/1509660899` got 0 from atan2(0,0). Reviews 10 and 11 found three more ways a facing could run along a bench or its own footway. It could follow another segment of that footway, or the next path on the same route, as `node/5759657845` did. It could also follow a footway crossing a way between its nodes. Review 12 found two last forms. Rounding could pick the side of an exact tie. A zero-length footway segment gave 0 from atan2(0,0), the symptom `way/1509660899` had shown. Checks in `test/campus-environment.test.mjs` cover the class, each red with its defect put back. Numeric, numeric-string and compass facing put the back behind the seated person. Unknown-material points and ways are flat neutral markers on their mapped positions. A seat whose backrest tag is absent gets no back. A tagged back runs one side of a way, with shared corners at bends, and its top faces point up on either side. Seat walls close without a seam. The exported `estimateFacing` is tested on fixtures: a node on a footway, a way sharing a node with one, a footway crossing a way, a two-node way, and a path straight ahead of a way. The straight-ahead case is checked rotated, moved, and in projected 7-decimal coordinates, along with zero-length footway segments. It sets aside every path with a segment within 5 cm of the bench and measures a way from the middle of its length. It faces a way across its own line, so a way cannot face along itself. A node lying on a footway faces across that footway's nearest segment. At a bend or junction that segment can be the wrong one, so the node can face along the other; no published node lies at one. Every open mapped way's published facing is checked to cross its line.
