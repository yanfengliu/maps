## A lane probe calls the repo's pixel helper rather than re-deriving the stride

**Anchor:** `artifacts/inspection-final2/REPORT-satellite-overhead.md:31,141`, 2026-09-18, the satellite-overhead re-inspection. That lane's first `diff` blob matcher indexed a per-pixel mask with a **byte** offset, so it sampled every fourth pixel and reported **no difference at any of the fourteen terrain rims** — a plausible negative result that would have been written as "the cap changed nothing". A `pixel` probe disagreed with it at a named coordinate (a pixel at (1025,650) went `rgb(22,24,33)` to `rgb(42,41,42)`, delta 20); the lane fixed the index to `mask[offset / 4]` and re-ran, and every number it published is from the fixed run, with the fault disclosed. This is the **second** instance of the byte/pixel-stride class in this repository after `tools/flicker/judge.ts`'s `channelDelta` (see the defect register's flicker entry), and it arrived in a different author's instrument *after* the original was found and fixed. The failure mode is specific and worth naming: the wrong instrument reports a **negative** — absence of change — which reads as evidence of absence rather than as a broken ruler.

**Gate:** **owed, not built.** The mutation it must catch is preserved by the class itself: index a per-pixel mask with a byte offset and the probe must go red on a known change. The rule that would have prevented it needs no new arithmetic — the repository already has five adjacent pixel-difference helpers that multiply by four, verified by round 35 at `tools/post-chain/motion.spec.ts:94`, `tools/style-ui/support.ts:371`, `tools/flythrough/flythrough.spec.ts:240`, `tools/populated/diff.ts:49` and `tools/render-defects/frame-regions.ts:119` — so a lane probe should **call one of them rather than re-derive the stride**, the same shape as the rule above. The counter-example is in the same repository: `tools/inspect/crops.ts:668-677` refuses by name to write crops into a directory that already holds a different frame rather than guessing at an ambiguous input. The rule is carried in `docs/policies/local-rules.md`.

## A probe that agrees with itself is not evidence

**Anchor:** `artifacts/inspection-final2/REPORT-satellite-plaza.md:153-155,181`, 2026-09-18. The satellite-plaza lane built a rim-projection instrument whose internal checks all passed — the camera target landed at exactly (640, 360), a point 10 m above the target landed higher, all 134 scene bounding boxes parsed as axis-aligned — and which nonetheless **disagreed with the frames**: under that pose the horizon sits within ~50 px of frame centre and Shibuya Scramble Square's roof projects to screen y ≈ −384, far above the frame, while the frame plainly has a tall building rising to the top edge on the left. The lane discarded the instrument, recorded that its output is not evidence, and rested the rims answer on the pixels alone. What settled the projection basis elsewhere was **agreement with recorded pixels and between independent instruments**: a sibling lane's own forward projector put H1 at (782.4, 318.4) at depth 609.1 m against this lane's (782, 318) at 609 m, all fourteen apexes agreeing to a pixel, and two lanes independently reproduced the pre-cap hole positions to about 1 px. The same lane's published-markings control is what chose the azimuth convention at all (four candidates tested, only one landed the known-visible zebra on bright pixels).

**Gate:** **owed, not built.** A projection or re-derivation instrument has to be validated against a recorded outcome, not against its own internal invariants: the mutation to catch is a self-consistent basis that disagrees with the pixels, and the check is that the instrument reproduces a known pixel or a second independent instrument's answer on the same input. Read together with "A diagnostic must re-derive the tick's own arithmetic" above: both are a probe that agrees with itself and is wrong.
